Chongqing

Cyberpunk city, hotpot capital, mountain drama

★★★☆☆🗓 2–3 daysUnique architectureBest hotpotSpicy!

✨ Why Chongqing?

Chongqing is China's most surreal city — a mega-city of 32 million people built on mountains where two rivers meet, and the trains run through apartment buildings. Photographers call it cyberpunk: buildings stacked on buildings, neon-lit bridges layered over the Yangtze, and an elevator ride that feels like a mountain hike.

It's also the birthplace of Chinese hotpot — the original, dangerously spicy broth — and it's famous for one of China's friendliest street-food scenes. Come for the impossible architecture, stay for the food, and leave wondering how any of it was built.

The story: Hongya Cave ('Red Cliff Cave') is an 11-story complex of traditional stilted houses built into the cliff above the Jialing River, completed in 2006. Its stacked wooden pavilions — styled after the old riverside dwellings that once filled Chongqing — light up at night into a glowing, multi-tiered pagoda-like structure that everyone compares to the movie Spirited Away.

Why it's a must-see: At night it's pure magic: golden lanterns, balconies full of teahouses and snack shops, and the Jialing River reflecting the whole glowing stack below. It's the definitive Chongqing photo — and inside, the fourth-floor food street is a full local snack market.

💡 Kat's tip: Come after 7pm when it's fully lit. The classic view is from across the river on the Hongya Cave side; from the Jiefangbei side walk down to the riverfront for the postcard shot. Free entry, busy on weekends.

The story: Jiefangbei ('Liberation Monument') is a 27.5-meter obelisk built in 1947 to mark victory in the War of Resistance, standing in the middle of Chongqing's busiest shopping district. Around it, the CBD mixes brand malls, street markets, and the city's famous '8D' pedestrian network of elevated walkways and underground passages.

Why it's a must-see: This is Chongqing's downtown — the place to feel the city's relentless energy, shop, and watch the crowds. Just steps away you'll find the Ronghui and Shibati old streets climbing the hillside, and the best rooftop bars with skyline views.

💡 Kat's tip: Stand at the monument at sunset, then follow the downhill stair-streets toward the river — the walk down Jiefangbei's hillside alleys is a mini-adventure in itself. Free.

The story: Chongqing hotpot was born in the 1920s when dock workers boiled cheap beef offal in a shared pot of spiced broth on the riverside. From that pot grew a culinary empire: today the city has over 30,000 hotpot restaurants, and the local saying goes 'a good hotpot is 80% oil' — a churning, blood-red cauldron of chili, peppercorn and butter that's the spiciest in China.

Why it's a must-see: This isn't just a meal, it's the city's identity — and it's genuinely different from the Sichuan hotpot next door: heavier, oilier, fiercer. Order the classic 'jiuge' (nine-grid) pot to divide your ingredients, and brace yourself for the legendary 'wei' (numbing) that follows.

💡 Kat's tip: Get the yuanyang (鸳鸯) half-mild pot if you're not a veteran. Locals swear by dipping everything in sesame oil (it cuts the spice). Top spots: Qiuzui Hotpot, Xiao Longkan — ¥80–150 pp.

The story: At Liziba station, Chongqing's Line 2 monorail passes directly through the 19th floor of an ordinary apartment building. The station and the building were designed together in 2004 — the residents get soundproofing, and the train gets one of the world's most famous commuter routes, gliding out of a wall over the Jialing River.

Why it's a must-see: It's the purest example of Chongqing's impossible architecture — a city where a train is your upstairs neighbor. Watch from the observation platform below as the monorail disappears into the building, and ride the line yourself for the river-and-cliff views.

💡 Kat's tip: Take Line 2 and ride a couple of stops — the river crossing is beautiful. The best photo spot is the plaza across the street from Liziba station. Free; metro ride ~3 RMB.

The story: Ciqikou ('Porcelain Port') is a hilltop town that has stood above the Jialing River for over 1,000 years, thriving as a porcelain-making and shipping port since the Ming dynasty. Its stone lanes, teahouses and riverside cliff paths survived the 20th century largely intact, and it's now Chongqing's best-preserved old town.

Why it's a must-see: It's a glimpse of the river-trade Chongqing that existed before the skyscrapers: Ming-style gateways, Sichuan opera stages, teahouses on the cliff edge, and the best place in the city to shop for handmade porcelain and watch calligraphers work.

💡 Kat's tip: Go on a weekday morning before the tour groups. Walk downhill to the river for the old ferry view, then climb back up for lunch at one of the cliffside teahouses. Free entry.

🛏 Where to Stay

Pick the right district and half your trip is planned. These are the bases locals actually recommend for visitors.

Jiefangbei CBD Best for first-timers

Chongqing's center: the Liberation Monument, shopping, the metro hub and Hongya Cave are all within walking distance (or a 10-minute taxi). Everything on your list is 15–30 minutes away by metro or taxi.

🚇 Lines 1/2/6 · Xiaoshizi / Jiefangbei 🏛 Jiefangbei, Hongya Cave, Yangtze cable car
e.g. Niccolo Chongqing (sky-high luxury in IFS Tower) · JI Hotel (great value near Jiefangbei)
Nanbin Road (South Bank) Skyline views

Across the Yangtze, the Nanbin Road waterfront looks directly at the downtown skyline — the best night views in the city, plus riverside restaurants and bars. Slightly less convenient, considerably more romantic.

🚇 Line 6/环线 · Nanbin Road 🏛 Yangtze cable car, Danzishi old street
e.g. Chongqing Marriott (river-view rooms) · mid-range riverside hotels
Guanyinqiao Foodies & shoppers

Chongqing's second downtown and unofficial food capital: the Guanyinqiao night market and the surrounding streets hold some of the city's best hotpot and snacks, with direct metro access to the center in 15 minutes.

🚇 Lines 3/9 · Guanyinqiao 🏛 Guanyinqiao night market, 9 Streets food alley
e.g. Holiday Inn Express · local chains (very good value)
Ciqikou area Old-town atmosphere

Stay near the ancient town for riverside teahouses and cliff paths in the morning before the crowds. It's a 30–40 minute metro ride to the CBD, but you wake up in old Chongqing.

🚇 Line 1 · Ciqikou 🏛 Ciqikou old town, Jialing River
e.g. boutique guesthouses near the old town gate

🍜 What to Eat

Chongqing Hotpot 重庆火锅

The original — a churning cauldron of chili oil and peppercorns born on the docks. Fiercer, oilier and more addictive than anywhere else on earth.

🍽 🍽 Qiuzui Hotpot (秋咀) · Xiao Longkan · ¥80–150 pp
Xiaomian (little noodles) 小面

Chongqing's street breakfast: simple noodles in chili oil, vinegar and crushed peanuts — deceptively plain, devastatingly good. The city's answer to 'how to start the day'.

🍽 🍽 Any xiaomian shop with a queue · ¥8–15
Suanlafen 酸辣粉

Hot and sour glass noodles with peanuts, pickled vegetables and chili — street food perfection, usually eaten standing up from a paper bowl.

🍽 🍽 Jiefangbei & Ciqikou street stalls · ¥10–15
Maoxuewang 毛血旺

A fiery Sichuan-Chongqing classic: duck blood, tripe and vegetables swimming in a vast bowl of chili and peppercorn oil. Adventurous, and unforgettable.

🍽 🍽 Sichuan restaurants citywide · ¥60–100

💡 Kat's Insider Tips

Kat Says
Chongqing is built on mountains — you WILL get lost. Google Maps doesn't work well here; use Amap (高德) or Baidu Maps, and remember that 'two blocks away' might mean a 20-story climb.
Kat Says
The Yangtze River cable car (2 RMB) across the river is a working commuter line, not a tourist ride — do it at sunset for the best skyline approach.
Kat Says
Order hotpot with 'weila' (微辣) if you're not a spice veteran, and always get the sesame-oil dip — it's not optional, it's survival.
