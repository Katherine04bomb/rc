Xiamen

Gulangyu island, sea breeze, colonial villas, street food

★★☆☆☆ 🗓 3–4 days CoastalIslandsFood

✨ Why Xiamen?

Xiamen is the Chinese city that feels like a seaside town you'd find in Portugal or Greece. Palm-lined boulevards, the sea on every horizon, a mix of Chinese and colonial-era architecture, and a pace of life that's famously slow — locals call it "the garden on the sea." It's also one of China's cleanest, most walkable cities.

The centerpiece is Gulangyu, a car-free island of 19th-century villas and gardens that UNESCO calls the best example of Sino-foreign architecture anywhere. But Xiamen is more than its island: a hilltop temple overlooking the campus, an art district carved from old factories, a ring road built for cycling along the ocean, and seafood that lands on the table hours after it's caught. Pair it with nearby Quanzhou for a perfect Fujian week.

The story: In the 19th century, after the Opium Wars, foreign traders and missionaries settled on Gulangyu, a small island a 5-minute ferry from Xiamen. They built hundreds of villas — British, French, Dutch, Japanese — alongside the local Fujian style, creating what UNESCO calls "the most complete and authentic example of Sino-foreign architecture in the world." The island also became China's piano capital; nearly every family owned one.

Why it's a must-see: It's a car-free, traffic-light-free world of narrow lanes, hibiscus-covered walls, and sea views at every turn. Climb Sunlight Rock for the panorama, wander past the restored villas of the old foreign quarter, and visit the world's only piano museum. It's touristy, but it's genuinely beautiful.

💡 Kat's tip: Book ferry tickets online days ahead — same-day slots sell out. Go early morning, or better, stay overnight on the island after 6pm when the day-trippers vanish and the lanes belong to the cats.

The story: Nanputuo — "Southern Putuo" — was founded in the Tang dynasty more than a thousand years ago and rebuilt in the Ming and Qing. Unlike most temples, it welcomes the sea: it sits on the hillside above Xiamen University, with Buddhist halls climbing toward the summit and ocean views from the top.

Why it's a must-see: It's one of the few temples in China where you can be praying in a thousand-year-old hall, then step outside to a panorama of the South China Sea. The incense, the carvings, and the free vegetarian lunch (donation only) make it a peaceful, authentic counterpoint to Xiamen's beaches.

💡 Kat's tip: Entrance is free. Go on a weekday morning when the incense is strongest. Climb to the top hall for the best view, then walk down into the campus of Xiamen University next door — you need advance registration to enter the campus itself.

The story: Shapowei ("sand-tail bend") was once the old harbor where Xiamen's fishing fleets landed. When the port moved, the decaying warehouses and boatyards stayed — and in the 2010s, artists, baristas, and vintage dealers moved in. Today it's a tangle of fishing boats and craft breweries, seafood stalls and indie galleries.

Why it's a must-see: It's Xiamen's creative heart and the most fun district to wander. Watch fishermen mend nets in one lane, then sip pour-over coffee in a converted warehouse two blocks away. The sunset from the waterfront terrace is the best free show in the city.

💡 Kat's tip: Come in the late afternoon — it's quiet, the light is golden, and the area comes alive at night. Look for the murals on the old factory walls and the mini art gallery on the second floor of the fish market.

The story: Xiamen's island ring road hugs the coastline for 43 km, and a dedicated bike lane runs right along the water — one of the most scenic urban rides in China. It passes white-sand beaches, the landmark "White City" statue, and the point where the island faces the Kinmen islands across the strait.

Why it's a must-see: Cycling the ring road is how you feel Xiamen's "island city" identity: sea on one side, mountains on the other, and a salt breeze the whole way. Even a short sunset stretch from the city center to Hulishan Fortress (with its famous 100-ton German cannon) is worth it.

💡 Kat's tip: Rent a shared bike and ride the section from Pearl Bay to the Ring Road music plaza in the late afternoon. The sea breeze is strong — bring a light jacket even on hot days.

The story: Zengcuoan began as a poor fishing village of stone houses and shrines, tucked into a cove on the island's southeast coast. As tourism grew, the villagers turned their front rooms into guesthouses and food stalls — and today the maze of lanes is a buzzing seaside hub that still keeps its temples and its resident fishing families.

Why it's a must-see: It's the most atmospheric of Xiamen's beachfront areas: sandy beach on one side, night market with grilled oysters and peanut soup on the other, and the old village lanes winding between. It's touristy but honest — the seafood is fresh and the atmosphere is pure coast.

💡 Kat's tip: Come at night for the food market — the grilled oysters (5 yuan a dozen) are the best value in Xiamen. The beach at dawn is nearly empty and lovely for a barefoot walk.

🛏 Where to Stay

Pick the right district and half your trip is planned. These are the bases locals actually recommend for visitors.

Zhongshan Road / Old Town Best for first-timers

Xiamen's historic core: the pedestrian Zhongshan Road, arcade buildings, century-old food shops, and a 10-minute walk to the Gulangyu ferry. Best for food and walking tours of the old town.

Gulangyu Island Most atmospheric

🍜 What to Eat

Shacha noodles 沙茶面

Xiamen's signature bowl: chewy noodles in a rich, nutty, slightly spicy soup made with shacha sauce (a Fujian-Southeast Asian satay blend). You pick the toppings — shrimp, squid, pork, bean curd — and they're blanched at the counter.

🍽 Wujiazhai shacha noodle shops · ¥15–35
Oyster omelette 海蛎煎

Fresh oysters folded into a glossy sweet-potato-starch omelette and fried until the edges crisp. Served with sweet chili sauce — soft, briny, and wonderful. The ultimate Xiamen street snack.

🍽 Night markets · old-town snack shops · ¥15–25
Peanut soup 花生汤

A sweet, milky soup made by boiling peanuts until they soften into a golden cream — served warm with a soft-boiled egg or fried dough. A century-old Xiamen breakfast tradition.

🍽 Huang Zehe Peanut Soup (since 1907) · ¥8–15
Grilled seafood 烤生蚝

At the night markets, oysters and shellfish grill over charcoal with garlic and chili — cheap, fast, and straight off the boats. A dozen oysters and a cold beer is the classic Xiamen evening.

🍽 Zengcuoan night market · ¥20–50
