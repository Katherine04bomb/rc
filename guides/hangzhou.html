Hangzhou

Serenity, West Lake, tea culture, easy day trips

★☆☆☆☆🗓 2–3 daysBeautiful & calmNear Shanghai

✨ Why Hangzhou?

Hangzhou is the city Chinese poets have been praising for a thousand years — Marco Polo called it 'the finest and most splendid city in the world'. The centerpiece is West Lake, a 6-square-km lake of temples, pagodas and willow-lined causeways that's been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2011 and the model for 'Chinese paradise' in countless paintings.

It's also the home of Longjing (Dragon Well) tea, China's most famous green tea, and it's just 45 minutes from Shanghai by high-speed train — which makes it the easiest beautiful-day-trip in China, and a perfect 2–3 day escape from the big-city pace.

The story: West Lake has been China's favorite landscape since the Tang dynasty, when poets and emperors shaped it into a garden of causeways, islands and pagodas — including the Bai and Su causeways, the Three Pools Mirroring the Moon, and Leifeng Pagoda on the south shore. It became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2011 as 'a masterpiece of landscape design that has influenced garden art across East Asia'.

Why it's a must-see: You can circle the whole lake (about 15 km) by shared bike in an afternoon, or take a wooden boat to the islands — either way you're inside the landscape that defined how China pictures paradise. The sunrise over Broken Bridge and the sunset from Su Causeway are the two classic moments.

💡 Kat's tip: Rent a shared bike (~1.5 RMB/30 min) or join a boat from the shore (¥50–150 for a private rowing boat). Go early — the lake at 7am with morning mist is the version the paintings are based on.

The story: Lingyin ('Soul's Retreat') Temple was founded in 326 AD by an Indian monk, making it one of China's oldest and most important Buddhist temples. It sits beneath Feilai Feng ('Peak That Flew Here'), a limestone crag studded with over 300 carved Buddha statues from the Five Dynasties and Song period — some over 1,000 years old — including the famous laughing Maitreya carved in 951 AD.

Why it's a must-see: It's the full Chinese temple experience in one hillside: incense smoke, chanting, centuries-old carvings in the cliffs, and the largest hall of its kind in China with a 24.8-meter wooden Buddha. The Feilai Feng grottoes are the artistic highlight — ancient sculpture in a forest setting.

💡 Kat's tip: Entry is 45 RMB (Feilai Feng) + 30 RMB (temple) — buy both. Go before 9am to beat the crowds. Budget 2–3 hours. The vegetarian restaurant inside the temple complex is excellent.

The story: Longjing (Dragon Well) is China's most celebrated green tea, and this hillside village — set among the terraced plantations of West Lake's south shore — is where it all began, with over 1,200 years of tea history. In spring (especially March–April), visitors can pick leaves in the terraces, watch master roasters fry them by hand in woks (the traditional '10 techniques'), and taste the first flush of the year.

Why it's a must-see: There's no more complete tea experience in China: the fields, the craft, and the taste of tea that emperors once reserved for themselves. The village teahouses serve fresh Longjing with spring water, and the terraced hills are gorgeous even outside harvest season.

💡 Kat's tip: Visit in late March to mid-April for picking season (pick-your-own experiences from ~100 RMB). Genuine 'Shifeng' Longjing is pricey (¥2,000+/kg for top grade); buy from a registered farmhouse, not the roadside sellers.

The story: Hefang ('Royal Street') and its continuation Qinghefang sit on the site of the Southern Song dynasty's imperial street, when Hangzhou was the capital of China (1127–1279). The restored pedestrian street is packed with traditional shops: silk, tea, medicine halls like the 140-year-old Hu Qingyutang pharmacy, and enough snack stalls to eat your way down the whole block.

Why it's a must-see: This is Hangzhou's old city in one walk — Southern Song history, crafts you can watch being made (paper-cutting, silk embroidery), and the city's densest snack street. It's also where the 2,000-year-old 'dragon-well tea ceremony' shops and the famous Quyuan Fenghe lotus area begin.

💡 Kat's tip: Go at 6pm when the lanterns come on. Try the 'ding sheng gao' (定胜糕) steamed cakes and the sweet-osmanthus lotus root. Free entry; the Hu Qingyutang pharmacy museum is worth 30 minutes.

The story: The original pagoda was built in 975 AD by the King of Wuyue — and immortalized in China's most famous love story, the Legend of the White Snake, in which a spirit is imprisoned beneath it. The tower collapsed in 1924, and the current pagoda (rebuilt 2002) stands on the ruins, which are preserved under glass so you can see the original foundations and a buried Buddha relic.

Why it's a must-see: The sunset view from Leifeng Pagoda's top floor is the classic Hangzhou photo: the whole of West Lake laid out below, with the city glowing behind the mountains. And the story attached to it — part fairy tale, part archaeology — makes it the most atmospheric of the lake's landmarks.

💡 Kat's tip: Entry 40 RMB, open until 8pm. Time your climb for 30 minutes before sunset. The elevator goes up (yes, there's an elevator in a 975 AD pagoda), so it's accessible to everyone.

🛏 Where to Stay

Pick the right district and half your trip is planned. These are the bases locals actually recommend for visitors.

Hugbin / Lake East shore Best for first-timers

The east shore of West Lake, facing the water: step out and you're on the lakefront promenade, with the metro, the old city and the shopping streets behind you. The classic Hangzhou base — walkable to almost everything.

🚇 Line 1 · Longxiangqiao / Fengqi Road 🏛 West Lake, Broken Bridge, Hefang Street
e.g. Four Seasons Hangzhou (lakefront luxury) · Midtown Shangri-La (landmark, lake views)
Yanggong Causeway / Lingyin area Peace & nature

The west and northwest shores are quieter and greener — hidden villas, teahouses and bamboo paths, 10 minutes from Lingyin Temple. Perfect for couples and anyone who wants the lake without the crowds.

🚇 No metro — taxi or bus to the lake edge (15 min) 🏛 Lingyin Temple, Su Causeway, bamboo groves
e.g. Amanfayun (ultra-luxury in a tea village) · Mejiawu farmstay tea houses
Qinghefang / Southern Song area Old-city atmosphere

The historic south: Hefang Street's food and crafts at your door, the canal old town, and the lake a 10-minute walk north. The most atmospheric budget-to-mid option, with the best street food nearby.

🚇 Line 1 · Ding'an Road 🏛 Hefang Street, Hu Qingyutang, Wushan Hill
e.g. boutique guesthouses on the old streets · West Lake Youth House (budget, friendly)
Qianjiang New City (east) Modern & convenient

Hangzhou's modern CBD along the Qiantang River: business hotels, the tide-viewing riverfront, and metro access to the lake in 20 minutes. Choose it for hotel quality and value over old-city charm.

🚇 Lines 2/4 · Qianjiang Road 🏛 Qiantang River, Hangzhou Grand Theatre, tide bore
e.g. JW Marriott Hangzhou · InterContinental Hangzhou (landmark tower)

🍜 What to Eat

Dongpo Pork 东坡肉

Melt-in-your-mouth braised pork belly, slow-cooked in soy, sugar and wine until it's glossy and tender — named after the Song-dynasty poet Su Dongpo, who served it while governing Hangzhou.

🍽 🍽 Lou Wai Lou (楼外楼, famous since 1848, lake view) · ¥50–80
Longjing Shrimp 龙井虾仁

Freshwater shrimp stir-fried with young Longjing tea leaves — delicate, fragrant and uniquely Hangzhou. The tea's slight bitterness against the sweet shrimp is the whole point.

🍽 🍽 Lou Wai Lou · Guohao Hotel restaurant · ¥80–120
West Lake Vinegar Fish 西湖醋鱼

Grass carp in a sweet-sour vinegar sauce with a hint of ginger — a Hangzhou classic that's an acquired taste (the fish is cooked whole, bones and all), but a must-try.

🍽 🍽 Lou Wai Lou · ¥80–100
Beggar's Chicken 叫花鸡

A whole chicken stuffed with mushrooms and ham, wrapped in lotus leaves and baked in clay — cracked open at the table with a mallet. Smoky, juicy and theatrical.

🍽 🍽 Hangzhou classic restaurants (Lou Wai Lou, Grandma's Home) · ¥60–90

💡 Kat's Insider Tips

Kat Says
Hangzhou is 45 minutes from Shanghai by high-speed rail (¥73) — perfect for a day trip, but stay overnight for the lake at sunrise and the teahouse afternoon.
Kat Says
West Lake is free to walk; only the islands, temples and pagodas charge. The sunrise walk from Broken Bridge to Su Causeway is the best free show in China.
Kat Says
March–April is tea-picking season AND the lake's prettiest weather — but also China's peak travel season, so book hotels a month ahead.
