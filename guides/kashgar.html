✨ Why Kashgar?

Kashgar is the farthest west you can go in China — a Silk Road city on the edge of the Taklamakan Desert where Uyghur culture, Central Asian bazaars and 2,000 years of trade history still fill the streets. It's the most different place in China from anything you've seen: adobe alleyways, mosques, melons piled high, and a Sunday livestock market that hasn't changed in centuries.

This is ★★★★★ difficulty — very little English, an overnight flight from the east, and a culture that rewards patience. But for travelers who make it, Kashgar is the single most memorable stop in the country: a living Silk Road museum that feels a world away from Beijing or Shanghai.

The story: The old city of Kashgar has stood for over 2,000 years as a trading post on the Silk Road, its maze of adobe alleyways and wooden balconies barely changed since the days when caravans rested here between China and Central Asia. Today some 70,000 people live inside its walls, in homes built like a honeycomb — flat-roofed, mud-brick, and connected by lanes so narrow that two people can barely pass.

Why it's a must-see: This is not a museum or a film set — it's a living neighborhood where children play in the alleys, craftsmen hammer copper in their doorways, and the smell of naan bread fills the air. Wandering without a plan is the whole point; every turn reveals a workshop, a courtyard, or a group of elders playing chess under a grape trellis.

💡 Kat's tip: Enter through the East Gate (the grand opening ceremony happens at 10am) and just walk. Greet people with 'salam' and a smile — locals are famously warm. Allow a full half day; the alleys are endless.

The story: Id Kah Mosque, founded in 1442 and enlarged over centuries, is the largest mosque in China — its courtyard can hold over 20,000 worshippers for Friday prayers. On Sundays, the square around it and the nearby livestock market fill with farmers selling sheep, donkeys, horses and camels in a scene that looks straight out of the 19th century.

Why it's a must-see: The Sunday livestock market is one of the great street spectacles of Asia: thousands of animals, traders slapping hands to seal deals, and food stalls cooking the morning's catch. And the mosque — with its yellow-tiled gateway and vast courtyard — is the architectural heart of Uyghur Kashgar.

💡 Kat's tip: The livestock market peaks before 10am on Sundays — go early, it's at the town's west edge (taxi ~15 RMB). The mosque is open to visitors outside prayer times (entry 30 RMB); dress modestly.

The story: Kashgar's craft quarter is a street-long workshop where Uyghur artisans practice trades their families have held for generations: hammering copper pots, weaving carpets, forging knives, embroidering hats and carving wooden instruments. Many workshops have been here since before the Silk Road caravans stopped using camels.

Why it's a must-see: You can watch a copper pot being born from a flat sheet of metal, smell the wool of fresh carpets, and buy directly from the makers — no middlemen, no factory replicas. It's the closest thing left to a genuine Silk Road bazaar, and the craftsmanship is extraordinary.

💡 Kat's tip: Bargain politely but fairly — these are working artisans, and the quality justifies the price. Copper teapots (~100–300 RMB) and embroidered caps (~30–80 RMB) make the best gifts. Ask before photographing the craftsmen.

The story: Naan — flatbread baked on the walls of a clay oven — is the staff of life in Kashgar, and every neighborhood has its bakery. The classic round naan is stamped with a pattern, brushed with sesame and baked in seconds at 300°C. Karim's Bakery in the old city is locally famous for the freshest loaves; pair one with salted milk tea or a Uyghur fruit-and-nut tea in a traditional tea house.

Why it's a must-see: There's nothing like naan straight from a clay oven at 7am in an adobe alley — crisp-edged, chewy-centered, dusted with sesame. And the tea houses of the old city, where men gather all day over melon seeds and endless refills of tea, are the social heart of Kashgar.

💡 Kat's tip: Naan costs 3–5 RMB — buy a few for the day. For the tea-house scene, look for one with carpets on the platforms and locals playing dap (drum); the Uyghur tea with almonds, raisins and rock sugar is the classic order.

The story: Kashgar's food is Central Asian to the core: lamb kebabs grilled over charcoal with cumin and chili, hand-pulled noodles (laghman) tossed with lamb and peppers, polo (pilaf) cooked with carrots and raisins, and grilled whole fish from nearby rivers. This is the food the Silk Road traded — and it's still cooked the same way.

Why it's a must-see: After weeks of Chinese food, Kashgar's meat-and-noodle cooking hits completely differently: smoky kebabs, fragrant pilaf, and the best bread in Asia. The night market near the old city's east gate is the place to graze from stall to stall.

💡 Kat's tip: Kebab stalls with long queues are the good ones — order 10 skewers and stand there eating them. Try polo (5–15 RMB) and laghman (10–20 RMB) from the old-city kitchens. Finish with a bowl of local yogurt.

🛏 Where to Stay

Pick the right district and half your trip is planned. These are the bases locals actually recommend for visitors.

Inside the Old City Immersive experience

Boutique guesthouses tucked into the adobe lanes — Uyghur courtyard houses with carved wooden balconies, where you wake up to the call to prayer and the sound of naan baking. The most atmospheric stay in China, full stop.

🚇 No metro — old city is walkable; taxis cheap 🏛 Old City East Gate, Handicraft Street
e.g. Kashgar Old City boutique guesthouses (traditional Uyghur courtyards) · from ~250 RMB
City center (People's Square) Comfort &amp; convenience

The modern center around People's Square and the Id Kah area: international-standard hotels, English-speaking staff at the better ones, and easy taxi access to the old city (10 minutes). Best choice if you want comfort after intense days.

🚇 No metro — taxis across town ~10–20 RMB 🏛 Id Kah Mosque, People's Square, night market
e.g. Radisson Blu Kashgar (international comfort) · local 3–4 star hotels
Seminary / old city west edge Budget &amp; backpackers

A cluster of budget hostels and guesthouses around the old city's western lanes — atmospheric, central and cheap. Shared bathrooms at the cheapest places, but you're right in the action.

🚇 No metro — walk to the old city 🏛 Seminary area, old city west gates, bazaar
e.g. Seminary Hostel (converted madrasa, atmospheric) · budget guesthouses from ~100 RMB
