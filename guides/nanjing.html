✨ Why Nanjing?

Nanjing is China's forgotten capital. Six dynasties ruled from here before Beijing, the first Ming emperor built his tomb on its Purple Mountain, and in the 20th century it served as the Republic of China's capital. Few Chinese cities pack so much history into such a walkable, leafy, and surprisingly relaxed package.

Today the city walls still circle the old center — one of the best-preserved city walls anywhere — while the Qinhuai River glitters with lantern-lit boats every night. Add one of China's greatest museums, the most photogenic weeping-cherry tunnel in spring, and a duck-obsessed food culture, and you have a city that rewards slow exploration. Nanjing is history you can actually touch.

The story: Sun Yat-sen is the father of modern China — the doctor turned revolutionary who ended 2,000 years of imperial rule in 1911. He chose Purple Mountain in Nanjing for his burial site in 1925. The mausoleum's 392 steps are said to echo the 391 million people of China at the time, plus Sun himself.

Why it's a must-see: The approach is the spectacle: a forested avenue of plane trees, a white-and-blue memorial rising on the hillside, and a climb that builds to one of the best views over Nanjing. It's a place of genuine reverence, not just sightseeing.

💡 Kat's tip: Book a free ticket on the official WeChat account in advance — entry is by time slot. Go early to avoid the tour groups, and pair it with Ming Xiaoling next door for a full Purple Mountain day.

The story: In 1398, the Hongwu Emperor — a beggar who rose to found the Ming dynasty — was buried here under Purple Mountain with 40 concubines and, legend says, 13 treasures. His tomb has never been opened. What visitors see is the Sacred Way: a 2-km avenue lined with giant stone elephants, camels, horses, and mythical beasts standing guard.

Why it's a must-see: The stone animals are ancient, weathered, and wonderful to walk among — China's own petrified parade. The architecture established the blueprint that later Ming tombs in Beijing (like the Thirteen Ming Tombs) copied.

💡 Kat's tip: Autumn is magical — the Sacred Way's maple and ginkgo trees turn gold and red in November. Budget two hours and wear walking shoes; the grounds are large.

The story: The Qinhuai River has been Nanjing's pleasure quarter for a thousand years — where scholars took exams at the Confucius Temple, courtesans performed on painted boats, and poets wrote about the lanterns reflecting on the water. The neighborhood was rebuilt in the 1980s but kept the old layout and spirit.

Why it's a must-see: It's the soul of old Nanjing nightlife. At dusk the riverfront buildings light up in gold and red, boats glide under stone bridges, and a massive food street sells everything from duck blood soup to candied hawthorns. Free to wander, endlessly entertaining.

💡 Kat's tip: Take the night boat tour along the river — 40 minutes, gorgeous lights, and very cheap. Eat at the side alleys rather than the main square; prices drop and quality rises.

The story: In the 1360s the Ming founder built a city wall around Nanjing that stretched 35 km — the longest ever built anywhere. It survives almost intact, and Zhonghua Gate is its most dramatic piece: a fortress with three layers of walls, 27 hidden chambers, and enough space to hold 3,000 soldiers defending the gate.

Why it's a must-see: You can climb the wall and walk for kilometers along the top, looking down at rooftops, canals, and temple bells on one side and modern Nanjing on the other. It's China's history you can literally stand on.

💡 Kat's tip: Rent a bicycle or just walk the section between Zhonghua Gate and the Confucius Temple — the views of the Qinhuai area are the best. Sunset is spectacular.

The story: Nanjing Museum is one of China's "Big Three" national museums, holding over 400,000 artifacts that trace Chinese civilization from the Stone Age to modern times. Its greatest treasure: the "Bronze Ox Lamp," a Western Han dynasty lamp where smoke rises through the ox's neck into the body's water chamber — ancient engineering disguised as art.

Why it's a must-see: It's free, air-conditioned, world-class, and (unlike Beijing's Forbidden City) rarely overwhelming in crowds. The Ming and Qing furniture halls, jade collection, and the art of the Republic period are particular highlights.

💡 Kat's tip: Reserve a free ticket on WeChat the day before. Give it half a day — it's huge. The museum café is decent for a lunch break between halls.

🛏 Where to Stay

Pick the right district and half your trip is planned. These are the bases locals actually recommend for visitors.

Xinjiekou Best for first-timers

Nanjing's central hub: a giant pedestrian square ringed by malls, restaurants, and a metro interchange that reaches everything in 20 minutes. Best for logistics, food, and first-time visitors.

🚇 Lines 1/2 · Xinjiekou 🛍 Malls & food streets
e.g. Jinling Hotel (landmark) · international chains on Zhongshan Road
Confucius Temple / Qinhuai Old-town atmosphere

Stay where the lanterns are: river views, night boats, and street food right outside your door. Livelier at night than anywhere else in the city. Best for atmosphere and night owls.

🚇 Line 3 · Fuzimiao 🏮 River views & nightlife
e.g. riverside boutique hotels near the Confucius Temple
Purple Mountain / East Gate Nature & quiet

A green, upmarket area at the foot of Purple Mountain, close to Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum and Ming Xiaoling. Leafy streets, fewer crowds, and easy access to the Nanjing Museum. Best for walkers and families.

🚇 Line 2 · Minggugong 🌳 Forested parkland
e.g. Purple Mountain resorts · university guesthouses

🍜 What to Eat

Salt-brined duck 盐水鸭

"No duck survives Nanjing" goes the saying. The signature version is poached in salt brine until the meat stays pink, tender, and clean-tasting. Eaten cold, it's the taste of every Nanjing family table.

🍽 Han Fu Tang · Shui Xi Men duck shops · ¥30–60
Duck blood vermicelli soup 鸭血粉丝汤

Nanjing's beloved breakfast: silky duck-blood cubes, glass noodles, fried tofu, and duck offal in a rich broth, finished with chopped cilantro and a spoonful of chili oil.

🍽 Lao Ye Yong · Ya Du Lao · ¥15–30
Beef potstickers 牛肉锅贴

Golden half-moons of pastry stuffed with juicy beef and scallion, fried until the bottoms crackle. Served with vinegar — a snack that defines Nanjing's morning streets.

🍽 Jin Xiu Wei · Lian Biao Su · ¥8–15
Red bean & osmanthus sweets 赤豆元宵 / 糖芋苗

Sweet red-bean soup with soft rice balls, and taro chunks in a silky osmanthus-scented broth. Nanjingers end many meals with one of these warm, comforting desserts.

🍽 Old dessert shops near Confucius Temple · ¥10–20

💡 Kat's Insider Tips

Kat Says
Summer in Nanjing is famously brutal — visit in spring (cherry blossoms on Purple Mountain) or crisp autumn instead.
Kat Says
The night boat on the Qinhuai River is the best-value experience in the city. Go after 7pm for full lights.
Kat Says
High-speed rail makes Nanjing an easy 1.5-hour day trip from Shanghai — but give it a full 2–3 days if you can.
