✨ Why Chengdu?

Chengdu is the only place on earth where you can watch giant pandas eating bamboo 20 meters away, then sit in a 100-year-old teahouse and sip jasmine tea for the entire afternoon — and nobody will think you're lazy. This city invented the concept of 'slow living': a laid-back rhythm, 8,000 teahouses, and a food scene that's officially recognized by UNESCO as a City of Gastronomy.

It's also the gateway to western Sichuan — the mountains, monasteries and gorges that make this one of China's most scenic provinces. But the city itself is the star: giant pandas at dawn, Sichuan opera at dusk, and a hotpot dinner in between that will test your spice tolerance and steal your heart.

The story: Opened in 1987, this 100-hectare research base has grown from six rescued pandas to a population of over 200 — the most successful panda breeding program on earth, and the model for panda conservation worldwide. Red pandas roam the same park, and the base has also released captive pandas successfully into the wild.

Why it's a must-see: Nowhere else gives you this access to giant pandas: you can watch them munch bamboo, climb trees and (in breeding season) cuddle their cubs, just meters away. The 8am feeding time is when they're most active — by 10am most are asleep and the tour buses have arrived.

💡 Kat's tip: Arrive at 7:30am opening and head straight to the Moonlight Nursery to see the cubs. Allow 3 hours. It's 20 minutes by taxi or metro Line 3 (Panda Avenue station) + shuttle. Entry 55 RMB.

The story: Wuhou Shrine was built in 223 AD to honor Zhuge Liang, the legendary strategist of the Three Kingdoms era, and Liu Bei, the Shu Han emperor — the only temple in China housing both a ruler and his minister. Next door, Jinli Street re-creates the old Shu kingdom's marketplace: lanterns, snack stalls, folk crafts, and nightly Sichuan opera with face-changing performances.

Why it's a must-see: This is the most atmospheric introduction to Sichuan's history and culture in one stop: a 1,800-year-old story told through temples, teahouses and street theater. The face-changing (bianlian) opera performers who swap masks in a blink are a genuine art form you'll see nowhere else.

💡 Kat's tip: Visit Wuhou Shrine in the afternoon (50 RMB), then walk into Jinli as the sun sets — the lanterns make it magical. Catch the opera/face-changing show inside Jinli (from 60 RMB) in the evening.

The story: Hotpot began as a working-class dish in Chongqing's docks and became Sichuan's culinary religion. The broth is a deep-red cauldron of chili, Sichuan peppercorns and dozens of spices, bubbling over a gas flame while you cook your own meat, vegetables and noodles. The numbing 'ma' of the peppercorn is what makes it addictive — and uniquely Sichuan.

Why it's a must-see: A Chengdu hotpot dinner is not a meal, it's a ceremony: the boiling pot, the dipping sauces, the cold beer, the hours of conversation. It's the definitive way to taste Sichuan food culture — and the spice level is adjustable, so don't be scared.

💡 Kat's tip: Order the 'yuanyang' (鸳鸯) pot — half spicy, half mild — if you're nervous. Go with locals if you can; they'll order the good stuff (tripe, pig brain, lotus root). Book ahead for dinner, especially on weekends.

The story: People's Park has been Chengdu's living room since 1911. Its centerpiece is the Heming Teahouse (鹤鸣茶社), founded in 1923 — one of the oldest teahouses in China — where locals sit for hours over a 15–30 RMB pot of tea, reading, gossiping, playing mahjong and getting ear-cleaned by wandering barbers with silver tools.

Why it's a must-see: This is the real Chengdu lifestyle, preserved in a public park: an entire city that has perfected the art of doing nothing in public. Watch the old men play chess, listen to the tea-water hawkers, get an ear massage — it's the cheapest, most authentic cultural experience in China.

💡 Kat's tip: Pay for your tea (¥15–30, jasmine or green), find a bamboo chair, and stay as long as you like — nobody rushes you. The ear-cleaning service (采耳) is 30–60 RMB and a surreal, wonderful experience. Afternoon is liveliest.

The story: These three parallel alleys — Kuan (Wide), Zhai (Narrow) and Jing (Well) — preserve the courtyard houses (siheyuan) of Qing-dynasty Chengdu. Restored in 2008, they now mix traditional teahouses and puppet shows with designer shops, craft beer bars and Sichuan snacks, all under the city's best-preserved old roofs.

Why it's a must-see: It's Chengdu in miniature: ancient lanes, modern energy, and a perfect spot to taste the city's snacks in one walk. The side courtyards hide some of Chengdu's loveliest teahouses and courtyard restaurants, far quieter than the main alleys.

💡 Kat's tip: Come in the late afternoon for golden light on the grey-brick lanes, then stay for dinner and drinks. Free entry. Try the 'san da pao' (三大炮) dessert — glutinous rice balls with a theatrical bounce.

🛏 Where to Stay

Pick the right district and half your trip is planned. These are the bases locals actually recommend for visitors.

Taikoo Li / Chunxi Road Best for first-timers

Chengdu's fashionable center: the open-air Taikoo Li mall (glass pavilions around a Tang-dynasty temple) and the Chunxi Road shopping street, with metro Lines 2 and 3 crossing here. Most sights are 10–20 minutes by taxi or metro.

🚇 Lines 2/3 · Chunxi Road 🏛 Taikoo Li, Daci Temple, Chunxi Road
e.g. The Temple House (iconic luxury inside Taikoo Li) · Buddha Zen Hotel (traditional courtyard, great value)
Wide & Narrow Alleys Atmosphere & food lovers

Sleep among the old lanes: courtyard hotels and guesthouses tucked behind the Kuanzhai alley walls, minutes from People's Park's teahouses and the city's best snack streets. Quieter and more charming than the shopping center.

🚇 Lines 2/4 · People's Park 🏛 Kuanzhai Xiangzi, People's Park, Wenshu Monastery
e.g. Courtyard hotels inside the alleys · Wenshu Monastery-area guesthouses
Wenshu Monastery area Budget & local life

Around the 1,400-year-old Wenshu Monastery, this older quarter has street markets, vegetarian restaurants, and Chengdu's famous Wenshu snack street — plus the cheapest decent hotels in the center. More authentic, less polished.

🚇 Line 1 · Wenshu Monastery 🏛 Wenshu Monastery, Wenshu snack street
e.g. Lazybones Hostel (backpacker favorite) · budget hotels along the monastery wall
South High-tech District Modern & transit-savvy

The modern business district south of the center: sleek hotels, international restaurants, and a direct metro link (Line 1) to the train stations and airport. Choose this if you prefer comfort and quiet over old-town charm.

🚇 Lines 1 · Century City / Financial City 🏛 Global Center (world's largest building), Century City
e.g. InterContinental Century City · international chains

🍜 What to Eat

Hotpot (Huoguo) 火锅

The bubbling cauldron of chili oil and Sichuan peppercorns — Chengdu's culinary religion. Cook your own meat and veg in the broth, dip in sesame-oil sauce, repeat until happy.

🍽 🍽 Shu Daxia (蜀大侠) · Xiaolongkan · ¥80–150 pp · book ahead
Dan Dan Noodles 担担面

The taste of Chengdu in one bowl: springy noodles in a spicy, numbing, peanut-flecked sauce with minced pork — street food elevated to legend.

🍽 🍽 Chengdu snack chains (Long Chao Shou, 龙抄手) · ¥12–18
Sweet Water Noodles 甜水面

Thick, chewy noodles coated in a sweet-spicy sauce of brown sugar and chili — uniquely Chengdu, and the perfect palate cleanser after the numbing stuff.

🍽 🍽 Snack shops in Kuanzhai & Jinli · ¥12–15
Mapo Tofu 麻婆豆腐

Silken tofu in a fiery, numbing sauce of minced beef and fermented bean paste — named after the pockmarked ('mapo') woman who invented it in 1860s Chengdu. A certified classic.

🍽 🍽 Chen Mapo Tofu (陈麻婆豆腐, original since 1862) · ¥30–45

💡 Kat's Insider Tips

Kat Says
Panda Base opens at 7:30am — be there at opening. Pandas are most active during breakfast and asleep by 10am; the tour buses arrive at 9:30am.
Kat Says
Sichuan's famous 'ma' (麻) numbness comes from the Sichuan peppercorn — embrace it. And learn one word: 'wei-la' (微辣) = mild, your hotpot safety word.
Kat Says
Teahouse etiquette: one pot of tea (15–30 RMB) buys you a seat for hours. People's Park's Heming Teahouse is the classic — arrive after 3pm for the liveliest scene.
