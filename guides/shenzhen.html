Shenzhen

China's Silicon Valley, beaches, and skyscrapers

★☆☆☆☆ 🗓 2–3 days Tech & modernNear Hong KongBeaches

✨ Why Shenzhen?

Shenzhen didn't exist 40 years ago. In 1980, it was a fishing village of 30,000 people. Today it's a megacity of 17 million, home to Huawei, Tencent, BYD, and DJI — the companies shaping the world's technology. If Beijing is China's history and Shanghai is its finance, Shenzhen is its future.

But Shenzhen isn't just glass towers and circuit boards. It's also where young Chinese come to start businesses, make art, and surf. The city has more parks than any other in China, a thriving craft beer scene, and some of the best beaches in the Pearl River Delta. It's efficient, green, and surprisingly laid-back for a place that builds the future.

The story: Ping An Finance Centre was completed in 2017 as the headquarters of Ping An Insurance, one of the world's largest financial companies. At 599 meters, it's the second-tallest building in China and the fourth-tallest in the world. The design by Kohn Pedersen Fox features a stainless steel facade that catches the tropical light.

Why it's a must-see: The Free Sky observation deck on the 116th floor (541 meters) offers a view that stretches to Hong Kong on clear days. The elevator reaches the top in 55 seconds. It's a visceral reminder of what 40 years of growth looks like — from rice paddies to clouds.

💡 Kat's tip: Book tickets online to skip the queue. Go on a clear day — tropical haze can obscure the view. Sunset is the best time.

The story: In 1989, a Hong Kong art dealer named Huang Jiang moved to Dafen with 20 artists and started producing oil painting reproductions for export. Today, Dafen is home to over 8,000 painters who produce 60% of the world's oil paintings — from Van Gogh replicas to custom portraits you can commission in 48 hours.

Why it's a must-see: It's the most surreal corner of Shenzhen. Narrow alleys are lined with open-air studios where artists paint Mona Lisas, Tibetan landscapes, and abstract canvases while you watch. You can commission a portrait from a photo, buy original art for under ¥200, or just wander and watch creativity happen at industrial scale.

💡 Kat's tip: Bring a photo if you want a custom portrait (48-hour turnaround). Haggle politely — prices are flexible. The best artists are in the back alleys, not the main street.

The story: OCT Loft is a converted factory complex in the Nanshan district, transformed in the early 2000s into Shenzhen's creative hub. It's home to design studios, independent bookstores, craft coffee roasters, and the annual Shenzhen Design Week. The Overseas Chinese Town (OCT) company developed it as a cultural counterweight to the city's tech obsession.

Why it's a must-see: This is where Shenzhen's young creatives actually hang out. The street art changes monthly, the cafés roast their own beans, and the independent music venues host bands from across China. It's the best place to understand that Shenzhen isn't just about making things — it's about imagining them too.

💡 Kat's tip: Go on a weekend afternoon when the creative market is open. Grab coffee at Something For or Trunk Coffee, then walk to the nearby Sea World plaza for dinner by the water.

The story: Opened in 1994, Window of the World is a 480,000 m² theme park featuring over 130 replicas of famous global landmarks at 1:1, 1:3, or 1:15 scale. The Eiffel Tower, Pyramids, Taj Mahal, and Statue of Liberty all stand within walking distance of each other — a surreal monument to China's 1990s fascination with the outside world.

Why it's a must-see: It's unapologetically kitschy and genuinely fun. The 108-meter Eiffel Tower replica has an observation deck, the nightly dance show draws huge crowds, and the "Around the World in a Day" concept is weirdly satisfying. It's also a fascinating time capsule of 1990s China's view of global culture.

💡 Kat's tip: Go in the late afternoon and stay for the 7:30pm dance show. Buy tickets online for a discount. It's busiest on weekends — weekdays are much more relaxed.

The story: Dameisha ("Big Meisha") is Shenzhen's most popular public beach, a 1.8-kilometer stretch of sand on the eastern Dapeng Peninsula. It was developed in the 1990s as a weekend escape for Shenzhen's growing middle class and remains the city's default beach destination — complete with a boardwalk, seafood restaurants, and water sports.

Why it's a must-see: Where else can you surf in the morning, tour a tech headquarters at noon, and eat fresh seafood by the beach at sunset? Dameisha is no Phuket, but it's real, accessible, and surprisingly lively. The water is clean(ish), the sand is decent, and the seafood restaurants along the shore are honestly good.

💡 Kat's tip: Take the metro to Yantian Port, then bus M437 or a Didi. Avoid weekends — the beach gets packed. For a quieter option, head to nearby Xiaomeisha ("Little Meisha") or Xichong.

🛏 Where to Stay

Pick the right district and half your trip is planned. These are the four bases locals actually recommend for visitors.

Futian 福田 Best for first-timers

Shenzhen's CBD and transport hub: the Ping An Tower, Convention Center, and direct metro to Hong Kong. Stay here for convenience, business hotels, and the best metro connections.

🚇 Metro Lines 1/2/3/4 · 🏙️ CBD & shopping malls
e.g. Shangri-La Futian · Four Seasons Shenzhen · Marriott Executive Apartments
Nanshan 南山 Tech & coast

Home to Tencent, Huawei, and the city's best coastline. OCT Loft, Sea World, and the Bay Area promenade make this the most liveable district. Best for creatives and tech visitors.

🚇 Metro Line 2 · 🌊 Coastal promenade
e.g. Hilton Shenzhen Shekou · boutique hotels near OCT Loft
Luohu 罗湖 Hong Kong border

The original downtown and main border crossing to Hong Kong. Older but packed with shopping, street food, and budget hotels. Best for day-trippers to HK and bargain hunters.

🚇 Metro Line 1 · 🛃 Lo Wu border crossing
e.g. budget chains near the train station · older but convenient
Shekou 蛇口 Expat hub

Shenzhen's most international neighborhood: foreign restaurants, international schools, and the ferry terminal to Hong Kong and Macau. Best for families and anyone wanting Western comforts.

🚇 Metro Line 2 · ⛴ Ferry to HK/Macau
e.g. Fraser Suites Shekou · Seaview hotels · mid-to-high range

🍜 What to Eat

Fresh Seafood 海鲜

Shenzhen's coastal location means incredibly fresh seafood. Restaurants keep live tanks of fish, lobster, and crab that you pick yourself. Steamed with ginger and scallion is the Cantonese way — and it's perfect.

🍽 Shekou Seafood Street · ¥150–400 pp · pick your own live catch
Chaozhou Beef Hotpot 潮汕牛肉火锅

Paper-thin slices of fresh beef, blanched for 8 seconds in a light bone broth. Different cuts have different textures — tongue, neck, brisket, tripe. Dip in shacha sauce. It's Shenzhen's most popular dinner.

🍽 Baheli · Chaoshan Beef · ¥100–200 pp · book ahead on weekends
Dim Sum 点心

Shenzhen does excellent Cantonese dim sum — often with shorter queues than Guangzhou. Har gow, siu mai, char siu bao, and egg tarts are the classics. Many restaurants serve all day.

🍽 Lei Garden · Tao Tao Ju · ¥50–120 pp · go before 10am for best selection
Street BBQ 烧烤

Late-night grilled skewers of lamb, eggplant, tofu, and oysters, dusted with cumin and chili. Shenzhen's street BBQ culture is thriving — find a plastic-stool joint after 10pm and eat like a local.

🍽 Any street with red-plastic stools after 10pm · ¥40–80 pp · cash or WeChat

💡 Kat's Insider Tips

Kat Says
