🛏 Where to Stay

Baima Tibetan Family Homestay
Sleep in a traditional wooden Baima house — home-cooked meals, bonfire singing, the real deal
Wanglang Eco-Lodge
Simple cabins at the edge of the panda reserve — wake up to birdsong and mountain mist
Pingwu County Hotel (平武宾馆)
Basic but clean county-town hotel — good base if you want modern amenities

🍜 What to Eat

🍽 Baima Roasted Whole Lamb
Seasoned with Sichuan peppercorn and mountain herbs, slow-roasted over an open fire — a feast
🍽 Highland Barley Tsampa (糌粑)
Roasted barley flour mixed with butter tea — the Tibetan energy food. Simple, nutty, warming
🍽 Wild Mushroom & Potato Stew
Foraged mountain mushrooms with local potatoes — earthy, hearty, and impossibly fresh

💡 Kat's Insider Tips

Kat Says
Pingwu Baima is REMOTE. The nearest big city is Mianyang (3–4 hours by car). Hire a local driver or join a tour — public transport is nearly nonexistent.
Kat Says
Best months: May–October. Winter roads can be icy and some villages close for the season. Spring brings wildflowers; autumn has the best forest colors.
Kat Says
Bring small gifts (tea, snacks) if you're staying with a Baima family — it's a beautiful custom, and they'll treat you like royalty in return.
