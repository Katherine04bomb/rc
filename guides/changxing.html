✨ Why Changxing?

Changxing is the anti-tourist destination: a county of tea terraces, bamboo forests and Taihu Lake shoreline that Chinese travelers have quietly loved for centuries — and almost no foreign tourists know about. It's where China's first imperial tea factory was built in 770 AD, producing Zisun ('purple shoot') tea for the Tang court for nearly 900 years across five dynasties.

And it's shockingly easy to reach: 25–35 minutes from Hangzhou and about an hour from Shanghai by high-speed rail. Come for a day of tea-picking and bamboo walks, stay overnight in a farmhouse among the terraces, and leave with the calmest photos of your whole China trip.

The story: Guzhu Mountain is where Lu Yu — the 'Tea Sage' who wrote The Classic of Tea, the world's first tea treatise — discovered Zisun tea over 1,200 years ago and recommended it to the Tang emperor. The terraced slopes still produce this prized purple-shoot tea, and you can walk the same fields, pick leaves in season, watch the pan-firing, and taste tea brewed with water from the nearby Jinsha Spring.

Why it's a must-see: It's the most authentic tea-culture immersion in China: the actual birthplace of imperial tea, with the terraces cascading toward distant Taihu Lake. In spring you can pick your own leaves and have them roasted on the spot — a hands-on connection to a 1,200-year-old tradition.

💡 Kat's tip: Visit late March to mid-April for the pre-Qingming harvest. Book a tea-picking experience through a farmhouse in Guzhu village in advance (~50–80 RMB per person). Wear sensible shoes — the terraces are steep.

The story: Dubbed the 'Oriental Amazon' by Chinese National Geographic, this wetland park spans 2,500 hectares of water forests, reed marshes and lotus ponds. A raised boardwalk threads through a flooded forest of dawn redwoods, where the rare reed parrotbill — nicknamed the 'panda of birds' — has been recorded, alongside egrets, wild ducks and mandarin ducks.

Why it's a must-see: It's a rare chance to explore a flooded forest by boat in eastern China — drifting between tree trunks rising from still water, with lotus blooms in summer and mist in the morning. Quiet, beautiful, and almost completely unknown to foreign visitors.

💡 Kat's tip: Combine the boat tour with the boardwalk walk — they show completely different sides of the wetland. Best light for photos is 8–10am when mist still hangs over the water.

The story: Built in 770 AD by order of Emperor Daizong, this was China's first imperial tea factory — at its peak staffed by over 1,000 craftsmen who produced Zisun tea rushed by fast horse to the Tang capital (modern Xi'an) in time for the Qingming festival. The current complex is a meticulous reconstruction of the Tang-style compound, with tea-making exhibits, a Lu Yu pavilion and authentic Tang-era cliff inscriptions behind it.

Why it's a must-see: This is ground zero for Chinese tea history — no other site in China connects you so directly to the Tang dynasty and to Lu Yu, who wrote The Classic of Tea while living on this very mountain. For anyone who loves tea, it's a pilgrimage.

💡 Kat's tip: Entry 60 RMB; budget 2 hours. Don't miss the cliff inscriptions (moya shike) on the trail behind the complex — they're genuine Tang and Song carvings. The teahouse on site serves fresh Zisun tea with the view.

The story: This 12.5-km valley in Xiaopu town holds over 30,000 ginkgo trees, including a 1,300-year-old 'Ginkgo King' and an 1,800-year-old 'Ginkgo Queen', threading through ancient villages and a Buddhist temple. A glass-bottom skybridge offers a panoramic view of the golden canopy.

Why it's a must-see: When the ginkgoes turn in November, this becomes one of the most photographed landscapes in eastern China — a 12-kilometer tunnel of yellow that rivals Kyoto's autumn temples, with a tiny fraction of the tourists. It's the single best reason to time your trip to autumn.

💡 Kat's tip: Peak color is mid-to-late November. Take the sightseeing mini-train (included in the 80 RMB ticket) to cover the length, then walk back through your favorite section. Stay overnight in a village minsu to see the corridor empty at dawn.

Why it's a must-see: Even if theme parks aren't your thing, the free nightly water-and-light show on the lakefront is the best free evening entertainment in the region. And it's the closest you'll get to luxury accommodation with a Taihu Lake view.

💡 Kat's tip: The light show starts around 8:20pm and is free to watch from the lakefront. With kids, the animal world (100 RMB) is excellent value. Avoid summer weekends unless you like crowds.

🛏 Where to Stay

Pick the right district and half your trip is planned. These are the bases locals actually recommend for visitors.

Shuikou tea valley (Guzhu village) Tea lovers & slow travelers

Dozens of family guesthouses sit among the tea fields and bamboo groves, ~20 minutes from the high-speed rail station. Many offer full-board packages — room + 3 home-cooked meals for 150–200 RMB per person per day. The best base for the Tribute Tea House and Guzhu Mountain.

🚇 No metro — taxi from Changxing HSR Station (~25 RMB) 🏛 Datang Tribute Tea House, Guzhu tea terraces
e.g. Changxing Qinghe Minsu (~150 RMB) · Tea Language Farmhouse (~200 RMB full board)
Taihu Lake resort area Families & resort lovers

The Longzhi Dream complex offers the widest range of accommodation — from a panda-themed family hotel to lakeside resort rooms — with the water park, animal world and nightly shows steps away. 15 minutes from the county center.

🚇 No metro — resort shuttle from the HSR station 🏛 Taihu Lake, Longzhi Dream, nightly light show
e.g. Longzhi Dream Joy World Hotel (400–700 RMB) · Animal World Panda Hotel (family, from 600 RMB)
Changxing county center Convenience & transit

The compact downtown near the HSR station has the usual reliable mid-range chains and the best local restaurants, with buses to all the scenic areas. The practical choice for a one-night stopover.

🚇 No metro — local buses and taxis 🏛 HSR station, downtown dining streets
e.g. Atour Hotel Changxing (~300 RMB) · New Century Grand (~450 RMB)

🍜 What to Eat

Taihu 'Three Whites' 太湖三白

The signature lake harvest: white fish, white shrimp and silver fish from Taihu Lake, steamed simply with ginger and scallion to highlight their delicate sweetness.

🍽 🍽 Lakeside farmhouse restaurants · ~60 RMB per dish
Silver Fish & Eggs 银鱼炒蛋

Tiny translucent silver fish scrambled with farm eggs — light, fluffy and impossibly fresh. The taste of the lake in every bite.

🍽 🍽 Most farmhouse restaurants in Guzhu village · ¥30–40
Changxing Dry-Tossed Noodles 长兴干挑面

Hand-pulled noodles tossed in soy and lard oil, topped with crispy fish or braised pork — a beloved local breakfast, and some say the best in northern Zhejiang.

🍽 🍽 Shixiong Noodle Shop (downtown) · ~15 RMB
Bamboo Shoot Braised Pork 笋干烧肉

Dried bamboo shoots from the Guzhu forests slow-braised with pork belly until the shoots soak up the rich sauce and the meat melts — a farmhouse classic.

🍽 🍽 Tea Language Farmhouse & Shuikou nongjiale · ~45 RMB
