✨ Why Xi'an?

Xi'an was China's capital for over 1,000 years across 13 dynasties — the longest of any city — and it's where the Silk Road began. Emperors ruled here when London was a village, and the evidence is still standing: the world's largest ancient city wall, pagodas built by a Tang-dynasty monk, and an army of 8,000 terracotta soldiers guarding a tomb for 2,200 years.

But Xi'an isn't only ancient. The Muslim Quarter is one of China's great street-food bazaars, the city wall is the best place in the country to rent a bike, and the metro now links it all together. History nerds, food lovers and first-timers alike leave impressed — it's the perfect second stop after Beijing.

The story: In 1974, farmers digging a well near Xi'an stumbled onto the greatest archaeological discovery of the 20th century: an underground army built around 210 BC for Qin Shi Huang, China's first emperor. So far over 8,000 life-size soldiers, 130 chariots and 670 horses have been found, each warrior with a unique face — no two are alike. The entire site, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1987, covers 56 square kilometers.

Why it's a must-see: This is one of the world's true wonders — a silent army that has waited 2,200 years for visitors, built by over 700,000 laborers. Pit 1 alone holds 6,000 warriors in battle formation, and the sheer scale makes you understand why the first emperor thought he could conquer death itself.

💡 Kat's tip: Go early (opens 8:30am) and head straight to Pit 1 before the tour groups arrive. It's 1 hour from the city — take public bus 306 (游5) from the train station for 7 RMB, not the 200 RMB tourist bus. Allow 3–4 hours.

The story: Xi'an's city wall was built 1370–1378 by the first Ming emperor, on foundations laid in the Tang dynasty. It's the best-preserved city wall in China — and the largest anywhere: a 13.7-km rectangle, 12–14 meters thick at the base, with a 12-meter-wide top and a moat on three sides. Four grand gates and 98 watchtowers ring the old city.

Why it's a must-see: Nowhere else in China can you cycle the full circumference of an intact ancient city wall. The 13.7-km loop takes about 1.5–2 hours on a rented bike, with views over tiled old-city roofs on one side and modern Xi'an on the other — especially magical at sunset.

💡 Kat's tip: Rent a bike on the wall (45 RMB for 2 hours, or 100 RMB deposit) — bikes are available at every gate. Go 2 hours before sunset and time your loop to finish as the lights come on. Entrance is 54 RMB.

The story: The Muslim Quarter has been home to Xi'an's Hui Muslim community for over a thousand years — descendants of Silk Road traders from Persia and Central Asia who married locally and stayed. Its maze of alleys behind the 14th-century Drum Tower is now China's most famous food street: lamb skewers, roujiamo, biangbiang noodles, dates, and dried fruit piled high under neon signs.

Why it's a must-see: This is where Xi'an's street-food soul lives. The main strip is touristy, but one alley deeper and you're eating where locals eat — hand-pulled noodles, bubbling paomo soup, and skewers grilled over charcoal. It's the perfect evening after a day of history.

💡 Kat's tip: Skip the shops right on the main street; wander the side alleys (Xiyangshi, Beiyuanmen) for better food and prices. Go at 6pm when it's lively but not yet packed. Bring small cash for the stalls.

The story: Built in 652 AD, this 64.5-meter, seven-story brick pagoda was commissioned by the great Tang-dynasty monk Xuanzang to store the Buddhist scriptures he carried back from 17 years of travel across India — the journey that later inspired the novel 'Journey to the West'. The surrounding Da Ci'en Temple was once one of the most important monasteries in China.

Why it's a must-see: It's a direct link to one of history's greatest travelers — and the pagoda itself, elegant and uncluttered after 1,300 years, is a masterpiece of Tang architecture. The Daming Palace ruins nearby show how vast Tang Xi'an was: the Tang capital was once the largest city on earth, with a million residents.

💡 Kat's tip: Come in the evening: the square in front hosts the famous music fountain show (usually 7pm and 9pm, free), and the pagoda glows gold against the night sky. Entry 40 RMB for the temple; climbing the pagoda is an extra 30 RMB.

The story: Xi'an's treasure vault: over 370,000 artifacts spanning from prehistoric times to the Qing dynasty, including Zhou-dynasty bronzes, Tang-dynasty gold and silver, and thousands of pieces unearthed right in the city. Its collection is so rich that only a fraction is ever on display at once.

Why it's a must-see: If you want to understand why Xi'an matters — and see the actual objects behind all that history — this is the place. The Tang gold and silver gallery alone is worth the visit, and unlike many Chinese museums, the collection is world-class even by international standards.

💡 Kat's tip: Entry is FREE but quota-limited: reserve online (official WeChat mini-program) with your passport at least 3 days ahead. Go right at opening (9am). Allow 2–3 hours; the Tang galleries on the second floor are the highlights.

🛏 Where to Stay

Pick the right district and half your trip is planned. These are the bases locals actually recommend for visitors.

Inside the City Wall (Bell Tower area) Best for first-timers

The historic heart: the Bell and Drum Towers, the Muslim Quarter's food alleys, and the old-city atmosphere are all within walking distance. Metro Line 2 runs along the wall's north-south axis, linking you to the train station and the Terracotta Army bus.

🚇 Lines 2/4/6 · Bell Tower 🏛 Bell Tower, Drum Tower, Muslim Quarter
e.g. Sofitel Legend People's Grand (1950s landmark) · Eastern House Boutique (great value near Bell Tower)
South Gate (Yongningmen) Quiet & upscale

South of the Bell Tower along the wall, this area has a more refined feel — trendy cafés, galleries and boutique hotels tucked into old courtyards, with direct access to the wall's South Gate bike rental and the Yongningmen metro.

🚇 Lines 2 · Yongningmen 🏛 South Gate, Shuyuanmen old street, De Fu Xiang
e.g. Somerset Xindicheng Serviced Residence (apartments) · boutique courtyard hotels
Qujiang New District Sightseers & evening shows

The modern cultural district around the Big Wild Goose Pagoda and the Tang Paradise park — wide avenues, night fountain shows, and the city's most impressive modern architecture. About 15–20 minutes by taxi to the old city.

🚇 Lines 3/4 · Dayanta 🏛 Big Wild Goose Pagoda, Tang Paradise, Qujiang pool
e.g. Wyndham Grand Xi'an South (5-star near the pagoda) · mid-range chains along Yanta Road
Xi'an North Railway Station area High-speed train transfer

If you're arriving by bullet train (from Beijing 4.5h, Chengdu 3h) or flying in, the north station area is modern, cheap and convenient — though it's a 30-minute metro ride from the old city's sights.

🚇 Lines 2/4 · North Railway Station 🏛 High-speed rail hub, 3-km to airport bus
e.g. Xi'an Jinhua Hotel · business chains (Holiday Inn Express)

🍜 What to Eat

Roujiamo 肉夹馍

Spiced braised pork, chopped fine and stuffed into a crisp, griddled flatbread — often called the world's first hamburger. Xi'an's most iconic snack.

🍽 🍽 Muslim Quarter stalls · ¥10–15 each · order the 'lean' (纯瘦) or 'half-fat' (肥瘦) version
Yangrou Paomo 羊肉泡馍

The soul food of Xi'an: you tear flatbread into crumbs, the kitchen simmers it in rich lamb broth, and you eat it with pickled garlic and chili — a ritual meal that takes patience and rewards it.

🍽 🍽 Lao Sun Jia (老孙家) or local paomo shops · ¥30–50
Biangbiang Noodles biangbiang面

Hand-ripped belt noodles as wide as your palm, tossed with chili oil, garlic and vinegar. The name's Chinese character has 58 strokes — the most complex in the language.

🍽 🍽 Muslim Quarter noodle houses · ¥15–25
Lamb Skewers 羊肉串

Charcoal-grilled lamb skewers dusted with cumin and chili — the soundtrack of the Muslim Quarter at night. Order them in tens, eat them standing up.

🍽 🍽 Beiyuanmen alley, Muslim Quarter · ¥3–5 per skewer
