Sanya

Tropical beaches, palm trees, resorts, island escapes

★★☆☆☆ 🗓 3–5 days BeachesResortsTropical

✨ Why Sanya?

Sanya is China's Hawaii — the country's only true tropical beach destination, sitting at the southern tip of Hainan island at the same latitude as Hawaii. Coconut palms line white-sand bays, the sea stays swimmable almost year-round, and the air smells of jasmine and salt. If your China trip has been all temples and noodles, this is where you exhale.

It's not just a resort bubble, though. Sanya has the 108-meter Guanyin statue on its own island, a bay where the mountains meet the sea at "the Ends of the Earth," and a local culture with deep Li and Miao minority roots. Pick your bay by mood — party, family, or quiet luxury — and let the ocean do the rest.

The story: Yalong Bay ("Dragon Tooth Bay") is a crescent of white coral sand and shallow, calm water — widely rated the best beach in China. Mountains shelter it on three sides, and the resort strip behind the sand is where Sanya's luxury hotels line up like pearls. Locals say the sand here squeaks under your feet because it's almost pure ground coral.

Why it's a must-see: If you came to Sanya for a beach, this is the beach. The water is clear enough to see your toes, the sunsets are long and gold, and even the most expensive hotels can't stop you walking their shoreline — in China, all beaches are public.

💡 Kat's tip: The central beach park (Yalong Bay Tropical Paradise Forest Park nearby offers a glass bridge and the famous "Bird's Nest" views). Go early morning before 10am or after 4pm for the best light and fewer people.

The story: Nanshan ("South Mountain") is a vast Buddhist cultural park where the hills meet the sea. Its centerpiece is the Guanyin of the South Sea: a 108-meter-tall, three-faced statue of the Goddess of Mercy standing on an island in the bay — one of the tallest statues on Earth, visible from kilometers away.

Why it's a must-see: It's genuinely awe-inspiring — a colossal white figure rising from the ocean that feels ancient even though it was completed in 2005. The park around it has temple halls, a vegetarian restaurant run by monks, and the "Dragon Boat" beach. Plan for a relaxed half-day.

💡 Kat's tip: Take the electric shuttle inside the park — it's huge. Visit in the morning when the sea breeze is gentle. The vegetarian buffet at the temple (66 yuan all-you-can-eat) is excellent and a great value.

The story: Wuzhizhou ("Hedgehog Island") is a small island about 30 minutes by boat from the coast near Houhai village. Around it, protected coral reefs keep the water brilliantly clear — shades of blue-green you won't see anywhere else in Hainan. It's the island's nearest neighbor to the famed "Mermaid" diving spots.

Why it's a must-see: This is where you come for the water: snorkeling, scuba diving, jet-skiing, and a glass-bottom boat over the reef. Even if you just want to swim, the beach here is dramatically prettier than the mainland bays.

💡 Kat's tip: Book the ferry and activities online a day ahead, and go early — the island caps daily visitors. The coral is sensitive: use reef-safe sunscreen and don't touch the marine life.

The story: "Tianya Haijiao" literally means "sky's edge, sea's corner" — the old Chinese phrase for the end of the world. For centuries, this rocky stretch of coast was where exiled officials came to die, far from the capital's politics. Later the boulders engraved with "Tianya" and "Haijiao" became China's most romantic symbol: lovers come here to pledge that they will follow each other "to the ends of the earth."

Why it's a must-see: The coastline is dramatic — weathered granite boulders, coconut palms, and waves crashing against black rock. It's also where Chinese TV taught generations to dream of Hainan. Sunset from the rocks is the classic Sanya photo.

💡 Kat's tip: It's more symbolic than spectacular — budget 2 hours, go at golden hour, and skip the touts selling "love lockets." Pair it with the nearby Nanshan Temple for a full south-coast day.

The story: Dadonghai — "Big East Sea" — is the beach that put Sanya on the map in the 1980s, right in the city itself. It's the friendliest beach in Sanya: sun loungers, banana-boat rides, kite surfers, and families flying kites until the streetlights come on.

Why it's a must-see: It's the most accessible slice of the tropical life — walk out of your hotel, cross the road, and you're on the sand. The evening scene is unbeatable: locals playing volleyball in the shallows, seafood grill stalls lighting up, and beach bars with the music turned toward the waves.

💡 Kat's tip: The night market just behind Dadonghai is the place for street food — try the coconut-grilled everything. For quieter water, walk to the east end of the bay where the swimmers thin out.

🛏 Where to Stay

Pick the right district and half your trip is planned. These are the bases locals actually recommend for visitors.

Yalong Bay Best for luxury

Sanya's premium resort strip: five-star hotels with private beach sections, infinity pools, and the best sand in China. Slightly isolated from the city, which is exactly the point. Best for honeymoons and pure relaxation.

🏖 Private beach access 🚕 40 min from airport
e.g. Ritz-Carlton, Banyan Tree, boutique resorts on the bay front
Dadonghai Best value

The city beach: mid-range and boutique hotels a short walk from the sand, plus the best night market and nightlife in Sanya. Best for first-timers, families, and anyone who wants the city and the beach.

🚶 Walk to the beach 🍢 Night market nearby
e.g. mid-range beachfront hotels · guesthouses on the bay
Sanya Bay Best for long stays

The longest bay, stretching 22 km along the city's south coast, with a boardwalk, cheap seafood, and sunset views of the whole city skyline. Hotels are the most affordable in Sanya. Best for budget travelers and winter escapees.

🌅 Famous sunset boardwalk 🛫 Close to airport
e.g. affordable seafront hotels along the bay boardwalk

🍜 What to Eat

Qingbuliang 清补凉

The essential Hainan dessert: shaved coconut ice topped with red beans, taro, peanuts, jelly, and fresh fruit, drizzled with coconut milk. Cool, sweet, and the perfect antidote to tropical heat — you'll want one every afternoon.

🍽 Dessert shops across the city · ¥10–20
Hainan noodle soup 海南粉

Hainan's signature breakfast: thin rice noodles in a rich sauce or soup, topped with shredded pork, squid, peanuts, and a squeeze of lime. Add chili oil and a boiled egg, and you've got a proper tropical start.

🍽 Local noodle shops · ¥10–18
Seafood grill 海鲜烧烤

At the bay-front night markets, stalls grill everything the boats brought in that day — shrimp, squid, crabs, clams — with garlic and chili, plus coconut halves to drink from. Point at what you want; it'll be on your plate in ten minutes.

🍽 Dadonghai night market · ¥60–150 pp
Wenchang chicken 文昌鸡

Hainan's famous poached chicken — free-range, cooked until just done, and served with a gingery chili dip. It's the dish Hainan is proudest of, and it's everywhere in Sanya. Eat it with coconut-flavored rice.

🍽 Hainanese restaurants across the city · ¥40–80

💡 Kat's Insider Tips

Kat Says

Kat Says

Kat Says
