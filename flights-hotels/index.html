How to Book Flights & Hotels for China

Booking China travel as a foreigner has quirks that catch people off guard — some hotels won't accept foreign guests, some platforms don't take international cards. Here's my honest guide to getting it right the first time.

⏱ 8 min read📅 Updated August 2026✅ Foreign-card friendly

Booking Flights to China

The major entry airports are Beijing (PEK or PKX), Shanghai (PVG for international), Guangzhou (CAN), Chengdu (TFU), and Shenzhen (SZX). All are well-connected from Europe, North America, and Australia.

Google Flights — Best for finding cheap fares
Use Google Flights to search and compare prices across airlines. Once you find the best deal, book directly with the airline or through Trip.com.

🛫
Direct with Chinese airlines — Often safest
Air China, China Eastern, and China Southern all have English websites and accept international cards. Their direct routes are often cheaper than booking through aggregators.
⚠️ Tip: Tuesdays and Wednesdays tend to have lower fares on China routes. Avoid Golden Week holidays (Oct 1–7) — prices triple.
💡 Kat's Money-Saving Tip
Flying into a secondary hub like Chengdu, Kunming, or Xi'an sometimes is 30–50% cheaper than Beijing or Shanghai. These cities are just as magical and make great starting points for exploring China. Worth considering if budget matters.
⚠️ Don't Book Non-Refundable Flights Before Your Visa
If your country needs a visa, wait for approval before booking non-refundable tickets. Even "guaranteed" processing can take longer than expected (I have been there when flying to other countries).

Booking Hotels in China

⚠️ The One Thing Most People Don't Know
Not all hotels in China are legally registered to host foreign guests. Budget guesthouses and small local hotels often cannot legally check in foreign passport holders. Always book hotels that explicitly say "accepts foreign guests" or stick to international chains to avoid arriving at your hotel and being turned away.
🏨
International chains — Always safe
IHG (Holiday Inn, Crowne Plaza), Marriott, Hilton, Hyatt, and AccorHotels all operate widely across China and always accept foreign guests. They also have English-speaking staff, which helps enormously on arrival.
✅ Slightly pricier but zero risk of being turned away. Worth it for your first China trip.

❗️ Always bring your physical passport
You will need your physical passport to check in every hotel! But not every hotel asks you for a Chinese phone number.
💡 Save Your Hotel Address in Chinese
Always save or screenshot your hotel name and address in Chinese characters on your phone. Taxi drivers and Didi drivers may not read pinyin or English. Ask the hotel for their Chinese address card (名片) when you check in — it may save you every time you need to get back.

Kat's Honest Booking Tips

📅 Best Time to Book
  • Flights: 6–8 weeks in advance for best prices
  • Avoid: Chinese New Year (Jan/Feb), Golden Week (Oct 1–7), May Day holiday
  • Sweet spot: March–May and September–October
  • Hotels: book early for Beijing, Shanghai and attraction sites — they fill fast year-round
💳 Payment Tips
  • Notify your bank you're travelling to China before you go
  • Visa and Mastercard work on most international booking platforms
  • Amex can have issues — have a backup card
  • Once in China, pay with Alipay or WeChat to see setup guide
🛡️ Always Book Refundable (First Trip)
  • Plans change — choose free cancellation rooms
  • Especially important before your visa is approved
  • Travel insurance covers unexpected cancellations too
  • Use travel insurance — always worth it for China
📍 Location Strategy
  • Stay near a metro station — transforms your trip
  • Beijing: stay inside the 4th Ring Road
  • Shanghai: stay in Jing'an or Huangpu districts
  • Check walk time to nearest metro on the map before booking
