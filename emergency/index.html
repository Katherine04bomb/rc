Emergency Numbers & What to Do If Something Goes Wrong in China

The vast majority of trips to China go completely smoothly. But knowing this guide exists — and having it saved on your phone — means you'll travel with a calmness that actually makes your whole trip better. Read it once, save it, then forget about it and enjoy China.

Emergency Numbers — Save These Now

110
👮 Police
Theft, assault, anything threatening your safety. Text 12110 if you can't speak Mandarin.
120
🚑 Ambulance
State your condition and location clearly. Get travel insurance — one ambulance ride can cost ¥500+.
119
🔥 Fire
Call immediately. Give your exact address. Evacuate the building first.
122
🚗 Traffic
Road accidents in cities. For expressway accidents dial 12122.
12308
🌍 Foreign Affairs
Lost passport, consular emergencies, legal issues. Multilingual support available.
12367
📋 Immigration
Visa questions, overstay issues, entry and exit problems.

What to Do in Specific Situations

🪪 Lost or Stolen Passport

  1. 1Stay calm — this is manageable. It happens to travelers every day.
  2. 2Call 12308 (Foreign Affairs hotline) for guidance
  3. 3Go to the nearest police station and file a loss report (失物报案证明) — you'll need this document
  4. 4Contact your home country's embassy or consulate in China to apply for an emergency travel document
  5. 5Keep a photo of your passport in your cloud storage — makes the process much faster
Prevention
Before you travel: photograph your passport and email it to yourself + store in cloud. Done.

🏥 Getting Sick or Injured

  1. 1For emergencies: call 120 for an ambulance or take a Didi to the nearest hospital
  2. 2Look for 国际医院 (International Hospital) — they have English-speaking doctors
  3. 3Show your travel insurance card and policy number at reception — saves you enormous paperwork later
  4. 4Key hospitals with international departments: Beijing United Family Hospital, Shanghai United Family Hospital, Chengdu United Family Hospital
  5. 5Call your travel insurer's 24/7 hotline — they can coordinate payment directly with the hospital
⚠️ Why Insurance is Non-Negotiable
A standard hospital consultation costs ¥300–800. An overnight stay can be ¥3,000–10,000. Surgery? Potentially ¥100,000+. Travel insurance costs ~¥30–50/day. The math is obvious. → See our travel insurance guide

😱 Scammed or Robbed

  1. 1Get to a safe, public location first
  2. 2Call 110 (Police) or send SMS to 12110 if you can't speak Mandarin
  3. 3Go to the nearest police station to file a report — you'll need this for insurance claims
  4. 4Cancel any stolen bank cards immediately via your bank's app
Common Tourist Scams in China
Tea ceremony scam: "Friendly local" invites you for tea, then presents a ¥500 bill. Decline invitations from strangers leading you to private venues.
Art student scam: Student asks you to see their "gallery exhibition" — overpriced prints then sold under pressure.
Taxi overcharge: Always use Didi. If taking a licensed taxi, insist on the meter (打表).

🚂 Missed Train or Flight

  1. 1Trains: Go to the ticket office immediately — you can usually switch to the next available train for a small fee (¥5–50)
  2. 2Flights: Go to your airline's desk in the airport — policies vary. Some allow rebooking for a fee, others require buying a new ticket.
  3. 3Trip.com has 24/7 English customer service — open the app → Help Center → chat with agent
  4. 4Travel insurance with "missed connection" coverage pays for rebooking costs — another reason to always have it

📱 Lost Phone

  1. 1Use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another phone or computer to locate or remotely wipe
  2. 2Report to police to get the loss report needed for insurance
  3. 3Temporarily borrow a phone to contact your travel insurer
  4. 4Budget Chinese smartphones are available for ¥300–600 at local phone shops if needed
Prevention
Enable cloud backup (iCloud or Google) before travelling. Keep your emergency contacts in your wallet on paper. Old school, but it works.
