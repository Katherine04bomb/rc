How to Order Food in China Without Speaking Mandarin

Eating in China is one of the great joys of the trip — and you can eat magnificently without speaking a single word of Mandarin. Photo menus, QR code menus, translation apps, and a handful of key phrases will take you further than you think.

⏱ 7 min read📅 Updated August 2026✅ No Mandarin needed
🌶️ Kat's Honest Food Warning
Chinese food in China is dramatically different from Chinese food abroad — spicier, oilier, more varied, and infinitely more delicious. Sichuan food will make you cry (in a good way). Dongbei food will confuse you. Cantonese dim sum will change your weekend mornings forever. Stay open to everything.
1

Types of Menus You'll Encounter

📷 Picture Menus — Easiest
  • Most local restaurants have photo menus
  • Simply point at what looks good and hold up fingers for quantity
  • Works perfectly — staff are used to foreign tourists doing this
  • Smile and point confidently — it always works
📱 QR Code Menus — Most Common
  • Scan the QR code on the table with WeChat or Alipay
  • A digital menu opens — in Chinese, maybe with photos
  • Use Google Translate camera to read text
  • Some QR menus let you order and pay directly — no staff interaction needed
📋 Chinese Text Only — Use Translation
  • Open Google Translate or Papago → camera mode → point at menu
  • Instant translation — works offline with downloaded language pack
  • Baidu Translate is a local alternative (works without VPN)
  • Show the translated screen to staff — they understand immediately
🌐 English Menus — Tourist Areas
  • Available in major tourist areas and international hotel restaurants
  • Dianping app lets you filter "English menu available"
  • Prices are often slightly higher where English menus exist
  • Worth using on your first day while you find your footing

Essential Food Phrases

这个
zhè gè
This one — point at the menu while saying this
不辣
bù là
Not spicy — essential for people who cannot eat spicy! (my spicy level is 0/10)
少辣
shǎo là
A little spicy — if you want mild heat
不要香菜
bú yào xiāng cài
No coriander / no cilantro (this is very personal suggestion)
买单
mǎi dān
The bill please — or just tap your Alipay scan icon
好吃！
hǎo chī!
Delicious! — makes every staff and cook smile instantly
我吃素
wǒ chī sù
I'm vegetarian — note: may still include fish/egg
打包
dǎ bāo
To take away / doggy bag please

How to Find Good Restaurants

⭐
Dianping (大众点评) — China's Yelp
The most trusted restaurant rating app in China. Every restaurant is reviewed by real locals. Photos show exactly what the food looks like. Filter by cuisine, price, rating, and "foreign-friendly."
✅ Sort by rating — 4.5+ stars means genuinely excellent. The photos are honest. Trust them.
💬
WeChat Mini-Programs — Order Delivery
Search "Meituan" or "Eleme" in WeChat — these are China's food delivery apps. You can order from any nearby restaurant and have it delivered to your hotel or even your train seat!
💡 For hotel deliveries, give your room number. For train deliveries, give your carriage and seat number before the next stop.
💡 Kat's Restaurant Strategy
Find the restaurant with the longest queue of locals outside. Queue. Order whatever the table next to you is having. This method has never failed me in 20 years of eating across China.

Dietary Requirements in China

🥬 Vegetarian
  • Say "我吃素 (wǒ chī sù)" but clarify — Chinese vegetarianism may include egg and fish
  • Buddhist vegetarian restaurants (素食 sùshí) are strictly plant-based
  • Search "素食" on Dianping for vegetarian restaurants near you
  • Tofu dishes, vegetable dumplings, and stir-fried greens are always safe bets
🌾 Gluten-Free
  • Very difficult in China — soy sauce contains wheat
  • Rice-based dishes are safest (rice noodles, steamed rice, rice porridge)
  • Show staff a card: "我对麸质过敏 (wǒ duì fūzhì guòmǐn)" — I'm allergic to gluten
  • Bring a printed allergy card in Chinese for serious allergies
🥜 Nut Allergies
  • Peanuts are common in Chinese cooking — often hidden in sauces
  • Always ask: "有花生吗？(yǒu huā shēng ma)" — Is there peanut?
  • For serious allergies, carry a Chinese allergy card
  • Congee (rice porridge) and plain steamed dishes are usually safest
🐷 Halal / Kosher
  • Halal (清真 qīngzhēn) food is widely available — large Muslim communities in Northwest China
  • Search "清真" on Dianping for certified halal restaurants
  • Xinjiang and Gansu cuisine is predominantly halal
  • Kosher food is very limited — research carefully before travelling
