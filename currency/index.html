How Much Cash to Bring

💡 Kat's Honest Answer
Bring ¥300–500 cash ($40–70 USD) as backup. That's it. China runs on Alipay and WeChat Pay — you'll almost never need cash. But having a small amount covers the rare situations where digital payment isn't available: some rural markets, older vendors and emergencies.
🏦 Best Places to Exchange
  • Bank of China — best official rates, branches everywhere
  • Airport exchange counters — convenient but rates are worse (5–8% less)
  • ATMs in China — decent rates, ¥20–30 withdrawal fee typically
  • Avoid: hotel exchange desks, unlicensed money changers
🏧 Using ATMs in China
  • Look for ATMs with Visa/Mastercard/UnionPay logos
  • Bank of China and ICBC ATMs most reliable for foreign cards
  • Tell your bank you're travelling to China before you go
  • Withdraw larger amounts — fees are per transaction, not per yuan
  • Daily limit: typically ¥2,000–5,000 per withdrawal
⚠️ What to Avoid
  • Don't bring large amounts of cash — Alipay covers everything
  • Don't exchange money at unofficial street vendors
  • Avoid currency exchange at airports when possible (poor rates)
  • Old or damaged Yuan notes may be refused — use crisp notes
💳 Daily Budget Guide
  • Budget traveler: ¥200–350/day ($28–50)
  • Mid-range traveler: ¥400–800/day ($56–112)
  • Comfort traveler: ¥800–2,000/day ($112–280)
  • Transport between cities is the biggest variable cost
