How to Book Chinese Attraction Tickets on WeChat & Alipay

In China, you don't "buy a ticket at the gate" — you reserve online with your passport number days in advance. This babysitter-level tutorial walks you through the whole flow on WeChat and Alipay mini-programs, with screenshots for every step. Master this and 99% of China's attractions are unlocked.

⏱ 12 min read 📅 Updated August 2026 ✅ Passport-friendly 🎟️ Works for 99% of attractions
🔴 Read This First · China Ticket Reality

You Almost Never Buy Tickets at the Gate

China runs attractions on a real-name reservation system. Unlike Europe or the US, most popular spots in China don't sell walk-up tickets — you must book a timed slot online, with your passport, before you arrive. Here's what that means for you:

  • 🪪
    Passport is your ticket. Every booking asks for your passport number and name exactly as printed. No passport, no entry — even if you "have a ticket."
  • 📉
    Daily caps sell out fast. The Forbidden City releases only ~40,000 slots/day and they vanish in minutes. The Giant Panda Base morning session is gone by 8 AM.
  • ⏰
    Tickets drop on a schedule. Most spots open booking 7–8 days ahead at a fixed time (often 8 PM Beijing time). Miss it and you wait another day.
  • 📱
    It all happens inside WeChat or Alipay. Each attraction has its own "mini-program" — a tiny app-within-an-app. This guide teaches you how to find and use them.

1

Find the Mini-Program

A "mini-program" (小程序) is a lightweight app that lives inside WeChat or Alipay — no download needed. To book a ticket, you open the attraction's own mini-program and tap around. Here's how to find it:

  • a
    In WeChat: from the chat list, either tap the search bar at the top, or pull down from the top of the screen to open the mini-program search. Type the attraction's official Chinese name (e.g. 故宫博物院).
  • b
    In Alipay: tap the search bar on the home screen and type the attraction name + "预约" (reservation), e.g. 故宫 预约.
  • c
    The official mini-program usually appears as the first result with a verified badge. Tap it to open.
  • d
    Tip: once found, tap "⋯" → "Add to Favorites / 添加到我的小程序" so you can reopen it in one tap on booking day.
💡 Kat's Tip
Search in Chinese, not English. "Forbidden City ticket" won't return anything — but 故宫博物院 will. If you can't type Chinese, copy the name from Part 4's table below and paste it into the search box.
2

Trust It — Spot the Official "Blue V"

This is the step foreigners mess up most. Next to many search results you'll see a small blue badge (蓝V) — that means the account is officially verified. You want this one, not a reseller.

  • a
    Look for the blue "V" or "认证" badge next to the account name. On WeChat it's a small blue circle-V; on Alipay it's a blue checkmark.
  • b
    Tap the account → check the operator / 主体. It should be the museum, scenic-area administration, or a government culture-tourism bureau — not a travel agency.
  • c
    Avoid accounts that say "官方授权代理 / authorized agent" or show tour packages — those are third parties who mark up prices and may not guarantee entry.
⚠️ Watch Out
Scam mini-programs exist. If the price looks 2–3× the official rate, or it asks for payment before showing your passport details, close it. Book only on the Blue-V official account — Part 4 lists the exact name for each top spot.
3

Fill In Your Passport

Real-name booking means the ticket is tied to you. You'll add each visitor's passport info once, then reuse it. Get this right or you'll be turned away at the gate.

  • a
    Tap "游客信息 / Add Visitor" and choose document type "护照 / Passport."
  • b
    Enter your surname and given name exactly as printed on the passport, in Latin letters. If the form splits Surname / Given Name, put them in the right fields — don't mash your full name into one box.
  • c
    Type your passport number carefully — no spaces, match the machine-readable zone at the bottom of the page. Double-check before submitting.
  • d
    Add a phone number. If it demands a Chinese (+86) number, use your hotel's front desk number or a local SIM — see the eSIM guide. Some programs accept your home number with country code.
  • e
    Save the visitor so you can re-select them on future bookings with one tap.
💡 Kat's Tip
Add everyone in your group before ticket-drop time. On a hot drop (Forbidden City, Pandas), you won't have seconds to type passports — just tap the saved visitors and hit pay.
4

Book, Pay & Screenshot

Now the actual grab. Many spots open a calendar a week ahead; you pick a date and a time slot, then pay.

  • a
    Tap "预约购票 / Book Tickets," pick your date and time slot (morning/afternoon). Greyed-out slots are sold out.
  • b
    Select your saved visitor(s), confirm the price, and pay via WeChat Pay or Alipay (foreign cards work if you set them up — see the payment guides).
  • c
    On success, you'll get a reservation QR code / 预约码. Screenshot it immediately and save it to your photo album — signal at the gate can be weak.
  • d
    At entry: show the QR code + your physical passport. Staff scan both; the name must match.
⏰ The Grab
Be online 2–3 minutes before the drop time (Part 4). The moment the clock hits, refresh the date and tap fast. For the Forbidden City and Pandas, the first 5 minutes decide everything.

WeChat or Alipay? Use Both

Good news: almost every attraction runs on both platforms, so you're not locked in. Set up payment on each before you fly. Here's the difference:

💬

WeChat

微信 · the all-rounder
  • 🏛️
    Best for government museums — the Forbidden City, National Museum, and most provincial museums run their primary mini-program here.
  • 🔎
    Find it: top search bar, or pull down from the chat list to open mini-program search.
  • 💳
    Pay with: WeChat Pay — link a foreign card or use the Tour Pass card. Setup guide linked below.
  • 🌐
    More English-friendly labels on many official museum programs than Alipay.
🅰️

Alipay

支付宝 · the aggregator
  • 🎡
    Best for scenic areas & aggregators — Great Wall sections, parks, and "景区预约" one-stop booking live here.
  • 🔎
    Find it: home-screen search bar; try name + "预约". There's also a "出行 / Travel" hub.
  • 💳
    Pay with: Alipay — foreign cards bind easily via the in-app "Tour Pass." Often smoothest for visitors.
  • 🌐
    Has a built-in English UI toggle — great if your Chinese is zero.
💡 Kat's Tip
Install both apps and bind a card on each before landing. If one mini-program crashes during a ticket drop (it happens), you can jump to the other. Don't discover at 8 PM that only one is working.

ℹ️ The Other 90%
Lakes, city walls, gardens, temples, and most provincial museums simply open a 7-day calendar you can grab any time. Use the same four steps — you just won't be racing the clock. No need to memorize anything; copy the Chinese name, find the Blue V, book.

Common Mistakes & FAQ

The stuff that actually goes wrong for first-timers — and the questions I get every week.

⚠️ Mistake 1 · Name order flipped
Surname and given name in the wrong boxes. The gate scanner matches your passport exactly — a mismatch = no entry. Always mirror the passport's Surname / Given name fields.
⚠️ Mistake 2 · Wrong phone number
Some programs text a confirmation to a +86 number. If you only have a foreign number, use your hotel's front-desk number — they'll hold the message. Or grab a local eSIM (guide linked below).
⚠️ Mistake 3 · Paid but didn't screenshot
Payment succeeded, you closed the app, and the gate has no signal. Always screenshot the reservation QR the second you see "成功." A saved image beats a spinning loader.
⚠️ Mistake 4 · Booked the wrong branch
Shanghai Museum, the Pandas, and Zhangjiajie all have multiple entries/branches with separate reservations. Double-check which one you picked before paying.
Can I just buy a ticket at the gate? ▼
For the top spots — almost never. The Forbidden City, National Museum, Terracotta Army, and Panda Base are reservation-only, no walk-up sales. Smaller parks may sell at the gate, but booking ahead is always safer and often cheaper. Don't gamble your one day in Beijing on a hope.
Do foreigners really need to reserve, or is that just for locals? ▼
Everyone, including foreign passport holders, must reserve — the real-name system applies to all visitors. In fact, booking with a passport is often easier because you skip the domestic ID queue. Some spots even have a separate foreign-visitor channel.
Passport number or name in Chinese — which do I enter? ▼
Enter your name in Latin letters exactly as on the passport (Surname / Given name in the right boxes), and your passport number with no spaces. Never enter a Chinese translation of your name — the scanner reads the machine-readable zone, so it must match the English on the document.
Do I need to print or collect a physical ticket? ▼
No. Show the reservation QR on your phone + your physical passport. Staff scan both; that's your ticket. Keep the passport on you the whole visit — random checks happen.
How many people can one WeChat/Alipay account book? ▼
You can add multiple saved visitors (family/friends) and book them all in one order — just enter each person's passport info in Step 3 first. Limits vary by spot (often 5–10 per account per day), but a normal group is fine.
I typed my passport number wrong. Can I fix it? ▼
Usually you must cancel the order and rebook (refund the wrong one first). Some programs let you edit visitor info before payment only. If it's already paid and the slot is gone, contact the spot's official mini-program customer service — response is slow, so double-check before you pay.
What if tickets are sold out? ▼
Three tricks: (1) Refresh at off-peak hours — spots release a few returned tickets through the day. (2) Try the other platform (WeChat vs Alipay). (3) Book a guided-tour ticket via the official channel, which sometimes holds separate inventory. Last resort: go very early and ask the on-site service desk about same-day returns.
Can I cancel or change the date? ▼
Most programs allow free cancellation up to the day before (refund to your original payment). Changing the date means cancel + rebook — and the new date may be sold out. Read the refund rule on the booking page; some cheap tickets are non-refundable.
