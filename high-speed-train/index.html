China High-Speed Train Guide for Foreigners (2026)

China's high-speed rail is one of the best travel experiences in the world. Trains that connect Beijing to Shanghai in 4.5 hours, with seats wider than most business class flights, a dining car, and even food delivery to your seat. Here's everything you need to know to book and ride one confidently.

💡 G vs D vs C Trains
G trains (高铁) — fastest, top speed 350km/h, most modern. Book these for long distances.
D trains (动车) — slightly slower (250km/h), often cheaper. Great for medium distances.
C trains (城际) — intercity trains between nearby cities. Short distances only.

Seat Classes Explained

BEST VALUE
🪑

Second Class (二等座)

Base price

5 seats across (3+2), totally comfortable for journeys up to 5 hours. This is what most locals use. Reclining seats, power outlets, and a tray table.

Kat's Pick
Perfect for trips under 5 hours. Spend the savings on food!
RECOMMENDED
💺

First Class (一等座)

~1.5× 2nd class

4 seats across (2+2), wider seats with more legroom. Quieter carriage. Worth the upgrade on journeys over 4 hours. Outlet at every seat.

Sweet Spot
Best comfort-to-price ratio for longer routes.
👑

Business Class (商务座)

~3–4× 2nd class

Fully flat seats (2+1 configuration), complimentary meals, dedicated attendant. Like flying business class, but you can actually see the countryside. Perfect for overnight or 6+ hour routes.

Treat Yourself
Beijing → Shanghai business class is a bucket list experience.
🧍

Standing Ticket (无座)

Same as 2nd class

No guaranteed seat — but you can often find empty seats in 2nd class carriages. Only buy this as a last resort. Not recommended for long trips.

Avoid
Book in advance instead — standing for 5 hours is not fun (trust me, I did that before).

How to Book as a Foreign Passport Holder

The official booking platform (12306.cn) now has English system and accepts foreign bank cards.

  1. 1Go to 12306.cn or open the Railway12306 app on your phone
  2. 2Tap Trains → enter your departure city, destination, and date
  3. 3Choose your train — G trains are fastest, look for morning departures to maximise your day
  4. 4Select your seat class — window seat (靠窗) is worth selecting
  5. 5Enter your passport number exactly — this is your ticket ID in China
  6. 6Pay with your international Visa or Mastercard
  7. 7At the station: go to the ticket machine → select "Foreign Passport" → scan or enter your passport number → collect your ticket
⚠️ Always Bring Your Physical Passport
Your train ticket is linked to your passport number. Security checks at train stations require you to show your physical passport alongside your ticket. A photo of the passport on your phone is not accepted.
4

At the Station & On the Train

🕐 Arrive Early
  • Arrive at least 30 minutes before departure
  • Security check is mandatory — like an airport
  • No liquids over 100ml, same as flying
  • Large stations (Beijing, Shanghai) need more time — 45 min to be safe
🎫 Boarding Process
  • Find your platform number on the departure board (check-in opens 30 min before)
  • Show ticket + passport at the platform gate
  • Scan your ticket barcode to enter the platform
  • Your carriage number is on the ticket — look for signs on the platform
🍱 Food & Drinks
  • Dining car has hot meals, noodles, drinks — reasonably priced
  • You can order food delivery to your seat via Alipay or WeChat mini program — seriously! And someone will bring your food to your seat.
  • Bring your own snacks for long journeys
  • Hot water dispensers are at the end of every carriage (free)
🧳 Luggage
  • No checked luggage — everything goes in the overhead rack or under your seat
  • No official size limit but be considerate — large suitcases are common
  • There's a luggage area at the end of each carriage for large bags
  • Keep valuables with you — don't leave bags unattended
🥚 Easter Egg from Kat
On some G train routes, you can pre-order from local restaurants near upcoming stations (at least 1 hour ahead) and they'll deliver to your seat at a specific time. It's mind-blowingly futuristic.
