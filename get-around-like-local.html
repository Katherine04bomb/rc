China Transport · 中国交通

Get Around Like a Local

China's transport network is one of the most advanced in the world — but it can feel intimidating if you don't know the system. This guide covers everything you need, from the moment you land to your last day.

Part 1: How to Get to Your Hotel

You've just landed. You're tired. Here's exactly how to get from the arrivals hall to your hotel — without getting scammed.

⚠️ First rule: Ignore anyone in the arrivals hall who approaches you offering a "taxi" or "ride." These are unlicensed drivers who charge 3–5x the normal rate and sometimes take you to the wrong place. Use the app.
Method Best For Cost (Approx.) How to Use
📱 Didi (Recommended) Most travelers — easiest, safest ¥60–150 (~$8–20 USD) Open Didi → set destination to hotel name in English. Wait in the 网约车 zone (ride-hailing pickup area). Share your hotel address in Chinese with the driver. Pay through the app — no cash needed.
🚇 Airport Metro Budget travelers, light luggage, hotel near metro ¥25–35 Look for 地铁 signs in arrivals. Most major airports (Beijing, Shanghai, Chengdu, Guangzhou) have direct metro links to city centres. Buy a ticket at the machine — English available.
🚌 Airport Bus Heavy luggage, no rush, on a tight budget ¥20–35 Look for 机场巴士 signs outside arrivals. Fixed routes to major city hotels. Pay with cash or Alipay at the counter. Slower than metro but less hassle with luggage.
🏨 Hotel Shuttle International chain hotel guests Free–¥50 Check with your hotel before arrival — many international chains offer free airport pickup. Book at least 24 hours in advance.
At your hotel: Show your passport — hotels are legally required to register it. Some budget hotels don't accept foreign guests, so book international chains to be safe. Ask the front desk for the hotel's address in Chinese (useful for Didi). Connect to WiFi and test your VPN before heading out.

Part 2: How Many Ways Can You Get Around China?

China has built one of the world's most impressive transportation networks in just two decades. Here's a breakdown of every option available to travelers — from bullet trains that cross entire provinces in hours to shared bikes you unlock with a phone scan.

🚄 High-Speed Rail (高铁 Gāotiě)

China's crown jewel. The world's largest high-speed rail network — over 40,000 km of track connecting every major city. Beijing to Shanghai in 4.5 hours. Trains run at 300–350 km/h, are almost always on time (average delay: under 1 minute), and are cleaner and more comfortable than most airlines. How to book: Use Trip.com (English, foreign cards accepted) or the 12306 app. Bring your passport to collect the physical ticket at the station, or use the e-ticket gate. Classes: Second Class (comfortable, best value), First Class (wider seats, quieter), Business Class (flat bed, meals included). Tip: Book 1–2 weeks ahead for popular routes like Beijing–Shanghai or during holidays.

🚇 Metro (地铁 Dìtiě)

Every major city has a modern metro system — Beijing alone has 27 lines. Most ticket machines have English. You can also scan your Alipay or WeChat transport QR code directly at the gate (much faster). Fares are absurdly cheap: ¥3–15 per ride. Rush hours (7:30–9am and 5:30–7pm) get extremely crowded — avoid if possible. Stations are clean, well-signed, and have staff at 客服中心 (Customer Service) for questions. Tip: Download your city's metro map before your trip.

🚗 Didi (滴滴) — China's Uber

Works in English. Set your pickup and destination in the app — it's about half the price of taxis in most Western countries. Most city rides cost ¥20–40 (~$3–6 USD). Send your driver a photo of your exact location if you're on a busy street. Copy-paste this to them: "我是外国人，不会讲中文" (I'm a foreigner, I can't speak Mandarin). Cheaper alternatives once you're comfortable: 曹操出行 and 花小猪. Avoid street taxis at airports and train stations — they often overcharge foreigners.

🚌 Public Bus (公交车 Gōngjiāochē)

The cheapest way to get around — fares are ¥1–2 per ride. Buses cover areas the metro doesn't reach and are found in every Chinese city, from mega-cities to small towns. Pay with your Alipay or WeChat transport QR code, or get a physical bus card at any metro station. Use Amap (高德地图) to find routes — it shows real-time bus locations and estimated arrival times. Most big cities announce stops in both Chinese and English.

🚲 Shared Bikes (共享单车 Gòngxiǎng Dānchē)

China's streets are filled with dockless bikes — you'll see blue (Hellobike 哈啰单车), yellow (Meituan Bike 美团单车), and green (Didi Bike) ones everywhere. Scan the QR code on the bike with Alipay or WeChat to unlock — no separate app needed. Cost: ¥1–3 per 30 minutes. Best for short trips under 3km, exploring neighborhoods, or getting from the metro to your final destination. Important: You must park in designated zones (look for painted rectangles on the pavement) or face a ¥5–20 fine. The app will show you where the nearest parking zone is.

✈️ Domestic Flights

Best for very long distances (e.g., Beijing → Yunnan, Shanghai → Xinjiang). Book on Trip.com for English interface and foreign card support. Budget airlines include Xiamen Air, Shenzhen Airlines, and Juneyao Air. Arrive at least 2 hours before departure — security is thorough. Luggage: typically 20kg checked, 5kg carry-on. For distances under 1,000 km, the high-speed train is usually faster door-to-door when you factor in airport transfers and security.

🚕 Regular Taxis (出租车 Chūzūchē)

Available everywhere but not recommended for foreigners unless you speak some Mandarin. Drivers rarely speak English, meters can be "broken" for tourists, and Didi is cheaper anyway. If you must take one, have your destination written in Chinese on your phone, insist on the meter (打表 dǎ biǎo), and take a photo of the license plate before getting in.

Part 3: Smart City Navigation Tips

Having the right tools and knowing a few insider tricks makes a huge difference. Here's what I tell every friend visiting China for the first time.

🗺️ Use Amap, Not Google Maps
  • Google Maps data in China is inaccurate and outdated
  • Amap (高德地图) has real-time traffic, accurate walking paths, and indoor maps of malls and stations
  • Switch to English in Settings — most labels will still show in Chinese, but menus become readable
  • Works offline if you download city maps in advance
💳 One QR Code for Everything
  • Your Alipay transport QR code works on metro, bus, and even some taxis in many cities
  • No need to buy separate tickets or cards — just scan at the gate
  • Set it up in Alipay: tap Transport (出行) → add your city's transit card
  • Works in 300+ Chinese cities
📱 Save These Before You Leave WiFi
  • Screenshot your hotel address in Chinese
  • Download offline Amap for your cities
  • Save a photo of your passport on your phone
  • Bookmark your hotel's location on Amap
⏰ Avoid Rush Hour
  • Metro rush: 7:30–9:00 AM and 5:30–7:00 PM — trains are packed shoulder-to-shoulder
  • Road rush: 8:00–9:30 AM and 5:00–7:00 PM — Didi rides take 1.5–2x longer
  • If you must travel during rush, the metro is faster than any car
  • Weekend mornings before 10 AM are the quietest
