How to Use the Metro in China

China's city metros are modern, clean, cheap, and incredibly easy to use — even if you don't speak any Mandarin. Most stations have English signs and the ticket machines have an English option. Master the metro and you'll unlock every corner of the city for ¥3–10 per trip.

How to Buy a Metro Ticket

You have three ways to pay for the metro in China. Option 2 (Alipay/WeChat QR) is fastest once you're set up:

✅ Option 1: Ticket Machine (Easiest for First Timers)
  1. Find the ticket machine in the station — they're always near the gate
  2. Press the English button (usually top right corner)
  3. Select your destination from the map on screen
  4. Choose quantity (X ticket)
  5. Pay with cash (coins or small notes) or Alipay/WeChat QR, foreign bank cards work in some station.
  6. Collect your token — a small round plastic disc
⚡ Option 2: Alipay or WeChat QR Code (I would recommend Alipay - the fastest and easiest)
  1. Open Alipay → tap Transport or scan the QR reader at the gate
  2. Select your city
  3. A QR code appears — hold it to the scanner at the gate
  4. Gate opens — fare is automatically deducted at exit
No ticket machine, no queuing, no tokens. This is how locals do it.(And it also works on buses)
💡 Option 3: City Transport Card
Each city has a rechargeable card (Beijing: Yikatong, Shanghai: Jiaotong Card) available at the service desk.

Navigating the Station

  1. 1Look for the 地铁 (dìtiě) sign outside — red or blue depending on city
  2. 2Security check first — put your bag through the X-ray. This is mandatory at ALL Chinese metro stations.
  3. 3Tap or scan your token/QR at the entry gate
  4. 4Find your line and direction on the overhead signs — always shown in Chinese and English
  5. 5Board the train — announcements are in Mandarin and English
  6. 6At your stop, tap out with your token (insert into the slot) or QR scan at the exit gate
⚠️ Don't Forget to Tap OUT
With Alipay, the fare is calculated at exit — you must scan when leaving. If you forget, the system charges you the maximum fare (around 15 yuan/trip).

City-by-City Metro Tips

🏙️ Beijing Metro
  • 27 lines, 490+ stations — huge network
  • Line 1 and Line 10 cover most tourist sites
  • Airport Express: Line 10 + Airport Express (¥25, 30 min to Terminal 3)
  • Rush hours brutal: 7:30–9am, 5:30–7pm — avoid with luggage
  • App: Beijing Metro app (English available)
🌆 Shanghai Metro
  • 20 lines, 500+ stations — one of world's largest
  • Line 2 connects Pudong Airport to city centre (¥7, 50 min)
  • Line 10 is great for the Bund and French Concession
  • Clean, well-signed, easiest for first-timers
  • App: Metro大都会 (Shanghai Metro official)
🐼 Chengdu Metro
  • 13 lines covering the whole city
  • Line 10 goes directly to Tianfu Airport
  • Line 3 for Chunxi Road shopping and food street
  • Less crowded than Beijing/Shanghai
  • Bilingual signs throughout ✅
💡 All Cities
  • Fares: ¥2–15 depending on distance
  • All major city metros have English signs ✅
  • Alipay QR works in all city metros ✅
  • Free Wi-Fi in most stations (needs Chinese phone number to activate)
  • Lost? 客服中心 (Customer Service) is always staffed
