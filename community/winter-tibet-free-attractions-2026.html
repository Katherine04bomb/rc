Winter Tibet 2026–27: Free Entry Deals and What Foreigners Need First

📅 16 October 2026 CHINA NEWS
🇨🇳 China News — If Lhasa has been on your list but peak-season prices and crowds put you off, winter is the window. Here's what's on offer and the one thing foreigners must sort out first. THE DEAL The "Winter Tibet" promotion runs from 15 October 2026 to 15 March 2027. During this period, A-level attractions across the Tibet Autonomous Region are free to enter, except temple and monastery sites. Flights are cheaper too. Deals seen so far include Chongqing to Lhasa around ¥409 one-way, Kunming about ¥499 and Xi'an about ¥529, compared with roughly ¥1,800 for a direct flight in July and August. Free means the ticket only. Shuttle buses (for example at Namtso) and extras are still paid, and big sites like the Potala Palace still require advance, passport-based booking. The Potala Palace has been free to enter in past winter seasons, and the Lhasa Tourism notices point the same way this year. Check the official Potala ticketing mini program before you finalise dates, because rules and hours change. FIRST, THE PERMIT (FOREIGNERS ONLY) Foreign passport holders need a Tibet Travel Permit to enter the region, and you cannot apply for it yourself. It must be arranged through a licensed travel agency, and in practice you travel with a pre-booked itinerary and a guide. Without the permit, airlines and trains won't let you board for Lhasa. Start at least three weeks before you fly, get quotes from two or three agencies, and ask exactly what is included. WHAT TO SEE Potala Palace — The icon. Winter light on the white and red walls against a deep blue sky is the shot. Winter hours are about 9:30 to 15:20, closed Mondays. No same-day tickets. No photos inside. Jokhang Temple and Barkhor Street — The heart of old Lhasa. Barkhor Street is free to walk, and in winter it is mostly locals and prayer wheels. Temples like the Jokhang are generally excluded from the free list, so expect to pay at the door. Norbulingka and the Tibet Museum — Next-door neighbours. The museum is the fastest way to understand everything else, and it's warm and quiet in winter. Namtso — About two hours from Lhasa. The lake freezes into deep blue ice in winter. Go with a driver or tour and confirm road conditions first. A SIMPLE 4-DAY PLAN Day 1 — Land and rest. Easy walk only. Day 2 — Potala Palace with your booked slot, then Jokhang and Barkhor. Day 3 — Tibet Museum and Norbulingka. Day 4 — Namtso, only if you feel fine and the road is open. WHAT NOT TO DO - Don't try to book Tibet like a normal city trip. No permit, no Lhasa. - Don't fly in and go straight to Namtso. Lhasa is at 3,650 m, so give yourself one to two days to adjust first. - Don't believe "free on the 15th of every month" rumours. The real free window is the winter promotion, and you still have to reserve. - Don't underestimate the weather. Winter days in Lhasa can reach 10 to 15°C in the sun, but nights are freezing and the wind at the lakes is brutal. WHAT TO PACK - A windproof layer, hat and gloves - Strong sunscreen, sunglasses and lip balm (the UV is serious) - Flat, grippy shoes for the Potala steps - Modest clothing for temples: no shorts or bare shoulders - Your passport, for every booking and checkpoint Updated October 2026. Policies and hours change, so confirm with the official notice for each site before you travel. — Kat
