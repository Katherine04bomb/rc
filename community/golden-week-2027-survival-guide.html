Golden Week 2027: What It Is and How Not to Get Crushed by It

📅 30 Sep 2026 CHINA NEWS
🇨🇳 Kat's Hacks — Every China guide says "avoid Golden Week." Almost none explain what that actually means. Here's the real version, so you can plan 2027 properly. WHAT GOLDEN WEEK ACTUALLY IS China has seven public holidays. Three of them are long enough that the whole country travels at the same time: Spring Festival (Chinese New Year), Labour Day (around 1 May) and National Day (from 1 October). National Day is the one most people mean by "Golden Week." The problem isn't the length. It's that everyone is off in the same window, so train seats, flights and hotel rooms run out at the same moment. Attractions stay open. You are not locked out. You are just fighting for space and paying peak prices. 2027 WINDOWS TO PLAN AROUND Spring Festival — Chinese New Year is 6 February 2027. Crowd level: extreme. The biggest movement of people on earth. Labour Day — around 1 to 5 May. Crowd level: extreme. National Day — starts 1 October, usually a full week. Crowd level: extreme. The small ones — New Year's Day, Qingming (early April), Dragon Boat (June), Mid-Autumn (mid-September). Crowd level: moderate. China usually confirms the official calendar for the next year in November, including the weekend swaps that stretch or shift the break. Treat these dates as a guide and check the confirmed ones before you book anything. WHAT IT ACTUALLY COSTS YOU - Hotels in tourist cities are often two to three times the normal price. - High-speed rail tickets go on sale about 15 days ahead. The first and last days of a holiday sell out within minutes. Foreigners can book with a passport on Trip.com or 12306. - Popular museums and sights use timed, passport-based reservations. Slots vanish as fast as train seats. - Domestic flights spike in the same way. THE FIVE THINGS THAT GO WRONG AT FAMOUS SITES 1. Getting separated. Huge sites, many entrances, thick crowds. Agree a meeting point at every site and screenshot it. 2. Falls on the Great Wall. The steps are uneven and steep. Slow down, especially in wind or rain, and don't lean over railings for photos. 3. Crowd crush. It happens at entrances, stairways and photo spots. Keep to the side, don't stop in the middle of a walkway, and if the flow suddenly speeds up, plant your feet. 4. Exhaustion. 15,000 to 20,000 steps a day is normal at big sites. Split the day in two, drink often, carry blister plasters. 5. Theft. Phone in a zipped front pocket, backpack on your front, passport kept separate from your photocopy. WHAT NOT TO DO - Don't book the first or last day of the holiday. Those are the worst days for trains and roads. - Don't follow influencers to unofficial "hidden" viewpoints. No rescue, no safety management, no signal. - Don't arrive at a major site at noon. Be there at opening, rest in the afternoon, go back out late. - Don't rely on one payment method. Set up Alipay or WeChat Pay before you land and keep a backup card. MY PLAYBOOK IF YOU CAN'T AVOID IT Shift your dates if you can. The week before or after is dramatically calmer: about five days either side for National Day, ten for Spring Festival. Book the day tickets open, both directions at once. Swap the famous site for its less-famous twin. Almost every big attraction has one. Ask me and I'll tell you which. Save the numbers: 110 police, 120 ambulance, 119 fire. THE HONEST BOTTOM LINE Golden Week isn't a trap. It's a date. If you can be flexible, go in the shoulder weeks and you get the best version of China. If those are your only dates, don't cancel. Book early, start early, and plan around the crowds instead of fighting them. Not sure whether your dates hit a Chinese holiday?
