Do I Need a Plug Adapter for China? Voltage, Sockets and What to Buy

📅 16 Sep 2026 KAT'S TIP
China runs on 220V / 50Hz — not the 110V you get in the US or Japan. Before you pack, flip over your phone charger and read the tiny print: if it says "100-240V" you are fine with just a cheap plug adapter. If it says a single voltage like "110V", you need a converter, or the device may fry. Most phone and laptop chargers are dual-voltage these days, so for most travellers the answer is simple: bring a universal adapter, skip the converter. THE THREE PLUG SHAPES YOU WILL MEET Type A — two flat pins — phone chargers and small devices Type C — two round pins (same as Europe) — laptop and camera chargers Type I — three flat pins (same as Australia) — air conditioners and high-power appliances Good news: Chinese wall sockets are usually combo units that swallow both two-pin and three-pin plugs in one faceplate, so you rarely get caught out. WHAT TO BUY Get a universal adapter that switches between US, EU, UK and Australian pin types, and accepts both 2-pin and 3-pin plugs so it fits China's new national-standard sockets. Choose one that sits firmly. Cheap loose adapters wobble out of the socket mid-charge and are genuinely annoying. Budget 15 to 50 RMB if you buy inside China. Bringing several devices? Pack one adapter plus a small Chinese power strip, then charge everything off a single wall socket. FORGOT ONE? No need to panic-buy at the airport. Large supermarkets stock adapters, and online shops deliver fast — search 万能转换插头 ("universal travel adapter") on JD, Taobao, Pinduoduo or Meituan. On Meituan or Ele.me it can arrive in about 30 minutes. THE ONE REAL TRAP Hair dryers and curling irons. Many US and Japanese models are single-voltage AND high-wattage. Either bring a transformer rated for the wattage, or just buy a cheap one in China. WHERE TO CHARGE WHEN YOU ARE OUT Coffee shops, shopping malls and high-speed rail stations often have USB ports or shared power banks you rent by scanning a code. Hotel front desks usually keep spare adapters and chargers guests can borrow. PS — if you grew up in Hong Kong, Singapore or Malaysia, you already use the British BS1363 plug, which electrical engineers rate as one of the safest domestic plugs in the world: a fuse inside every plug, child-proof shutters, and insulated pins. The one known downside is how much it hurts if you step on it barefoot in the dark. Reportedly worse than Lego.
