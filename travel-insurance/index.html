Travel Insurance for China: What You Need and Why

This is the step most travelers skip. Then something goes wrong and they wish they hadn't. A hospital visit in Beijing without insurance can cost ¥5,000–50,000 ($700–7,000 USD). Travel insurance costs about $3–8 per day. The math is obvious.

⏱ 6 min read📅 Updated August 2026✅ From $3/day
💰 Real Numbers
Standard consultation at Beijing United Family Hospital: ¥800–2,000
Overnight hospital stay: ¥3,000–15,000
Appendectomy surgery: ¥30,000–80,000
Medical evacuation to home country: up to $150,000 USD

10-day travel insurance (World Nomads): ~$45 USD
1

What Your Insurance Must Cover

✅ Non-Negotiable
  • Medical coverage: at least $100,000 USD
  • Emergency evacuation: must be included — China to your home country is expensive
  • 24/7 emergency assistance hotline: in English
  • Hospital direct billing: so you don't have to pay upfront
👍 Highly Recommended
  • Trip cancellation/interruption
  • Lost or stolen luggage (¥500 minimum cover)
  • Passport/document replacement
  • Missed connections
  • Adventure activities (hiking, cycling — check if included)
🔍 Check Before Buying
  • Is China explicitly listed as a covered destination?
  • Are pre-existing conditions covered?
  • What's the deductible/excess per claim?
  • Does it cover COVID-19 related cancellations?
  • Does your credit card already include travel insurance? (Many do!)
⚠️ Common Gaps
  • Extreme sports often excluded — check if you're hiking or cycling
  • Some policies don't cover trips over 30 days
  • Pre-existing conditions often excluded without upgrade
  • "Cancel for any reason" is usually a separate add-on

How to Use Your Insurance in China

  1. 1Before travelling: save your policy number and the 24/7 emergency hotline in your phone contacts
  2. 2Print or screenshot your insurance card — keep it in your wallet alongside your passport
  3. 3When something happens: call your insurer's emergency line first — they'll guide you to the right hospital and can often arrange direct billing
  4. 4Keep all receipts and medical reports — required for reimbursement claims
  5. 5For international hospitals in China, show your insurance card at reception — many have direct billing arrangements
