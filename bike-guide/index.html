How to Use Shared Bikes in China

The best travel hack nobody explains properly. No separate app needed — here's how to unlock, ride, and return a shared bike, plus the rules that actually get enforced.

⏱ 6 min read 📅 Updated August 2026 🪙 From ¥1.5 per ride
💡 Kat's Tip
Shared bikes are how I get around my own city day to day — cheaper than a taxi, faster than walking. No deposit, no separate download if you already have Alipay or WeChat set up.

The 10-Second Version

1. No new app needed

Unlock bikes straight from Alipay or WeChat — they run as a mini-program inside apps you already have.

2. Scan to unlock

Point your camera at the QR code on the handlebars. About ¥1.5 for the first 15–30 minutes.

3. No license needed

Helmets are encouraged, not required nationally — though some cities mandate them for e-bikes.

4. Park in the zone

Return inside a marked parking zone or you'll get an automatic ¥5–20 fine deducted from your card.

1

You Don't Need a Separate App

This trips up a lot of first-timers. You don't need to install Meituan, Hello Bike, or Didi individually — all three run as a mini-program inside Alipay or WeChat, the apps you already have set up for payments.

  1. 1Confirm your Alipay account is passport-verified (Settings → Account → Verification — look for the green badge).
  2. 2Confirm you have an international card linked, or Alipay's Tour Pass activated.
  3. 3Open Alipay, tap the transport icon on the home screen, and select the bicycle option.

Prefer standalone apps? Meituan, Hello Bike (哈啰), and Didi (with Qingju/青桔 bikes) are all free downloads with English-language settings.

2

Know Your Colors

Yellow — Meituan Bike

Access via the Meituan app or WeChat mini-program. Widest coverage in Tier 1 cities.

Blue — Hello Bike (哈啰)

Access via the Alipay mini-program. Widest coverage overall — 400+ cities, including smaller ones.

Green — Didi Qingju (青桔)

Access via the Didi app or Alipay mini-program. Convenient if you already have Didi installed for rides.

Coverage overlaps but isn't identical — if you can't find one color nearby, check the map inside another operator's app before giving up.

3

Regular Bike or E-Bike?

Both exist side by side in most cities, marked separately on the in-app map:

  • •Regular pedal bikes — the standard option, roughly ¥1.5 for the first 15–30 minutes depending on city and operator.
  • •Electric-assist bikes — capped around 25 km/h, cost more (roughly ¥0.50/minute). Great for longer distances or hot weather.
4

Unlock, Ride, and Pay

  1. 1Find a bike on the in-app map — available bikes show as dots near you.
  2. 2Point your phone's camera or the app's scanner at the QR code on the handlebars or rear wheel.
  3. 3Tap "unlock" — you'll hear a beep and the rear wheel lock releases within seconds.
  4. 4Ride. Payment is deducted automatically from your linked card when you end the ride.
⚠️ You Need Mobile Data
You need internet to unlock and to end your ride. Sort out a China eSIM before you land — see our eSIM guide.
5

Return the Bike Properly

  • •End your ride inside a marked parking zone — an outlined box on the in-app map, or a painted zone on the sidewalk in big cities.
  • •Push the lock closed on the rear wheel manually, then confirm the ride has ended in the app before walking away.
  • •If you're outside a valid zone, the app usually warns you before letting you lock the bike — don't ignore that warning.
  • •Forgetting to properly end the ride keeps charging you, even after you've walked off — always confirm the app shows "ride complete."
6

The Rules That Actually Get Enforced

  • •No license needed. Shared bikes don't require a driver's license — regular pedal bikes and e-bikes both.
  • •Helmets are encouraged, not legally required for standard shared bikes under national guidelines. A few cities — including Hainan and Shenzhen — mandate helmets specifically for e-bikes, with fines of ¥50–200 for riding without one.
  • •Ride in the bike lane — to the right of car traffic, separated by a line or curb — not on the sidewalk.
  • •Parking outside a designated zone gets you an automatic fine, typically ¥5–20, deducted straight from your linked card.
  • •Basic accident insurance is included in your rental fee, usually covering up to around ¥100,000 for injury — this is not a substitute for real travel insurance. See our Travel Insurance guide.
💡 Kat's Tip
If you're unsure about helmet rules in your city, just wear one anyway — grab a cheap one at Decathlon or Miniso for ¥50–100.

FAQ: Common Shared Bike Questions

Is there a deposit? ▼
No deposit is required if your account is passport-verified with a linked card. This used to be a hassle for foreigners; it's largely been resolved by 2026.
What if I can't find a bike from the app I opened? ▼
Check a different operator's map — Hello Bike's blue bikes often reach areas Meituan's yellow ones don't, especially in smaller cities, and vice versa.
Can I use headphones or hold my phone while riding? ▼
Don't. Chinese urban traffic — especially e-bikes and scooters weaving between lanes — requires full attention. Keep your phone away and your ears open.
What's the actual cost for a normal day of use? ▼
Most short "last mile" trips from a subway exit to your hotel run 10–20 minutes, so you're typically looking at ¥1.5–3 RMB per trip — under a dollar even with several rides a day.
Do I need to worry about theft or leaving the bike unattended? ▼
Shared bikes are designed to be picked up and left anywhere within a legal zone — you're not responsible for guarding it. Just make sure the lock has engaged and the app confirms the ride ended before you walk away.
