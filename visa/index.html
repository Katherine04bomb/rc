The 3 Ways to Enter China

✅

30-Day Visa-Free

77 countries can enter without a visa for up to 30 days. No application needed — just show up with your passport. Valid for tourism, business, family visits, and transit.

⏱️

240-Hour Transit (10 Days)

Passing through China to a third country? 55 nationalities (including USA, Canada) can stay up to 10 days visa-free at 65 ports — perfect for a mini trip on the way somewhere else.

📋

Tourist Visa (L Visa)

If your country needs a visa, apply at the Chinese Embassy before you fly. The L (tourist) visa is the standard option. Processing takes 4–7 business days. Plan ahead.

How to Apply for a China Tourist Visa (L Visa)

If your country isn't on the visa-free list, don't worry — the application process is straightforward. Here's exactly how to do it.

1
Check your local Chinese Embassy or Visa Application Centre

Find your nearest centre at visaforchina.cn. In some countries (like the US, UK, Australia) you apply at a dedicated Visa Application Service Centre, not the embassy directly.

2
Prepare your documents

You'll typically need: valid passport (6+ months remaining), completed visa application form, passport photo, flight bookings, hotel bookings, and bank statements showing you can fund your trip. Requirements vary slightly by country.

3
Submit your application

Most countries require an in-person visit. Bring original documents plus photocopies. Pay the visa fee at the counter (card or cash — check in advance).

4
Wait for processing

Standard processing: 4–7 business days. Express service (1–2 days) is available at extra cost. Don't book non-refundable flights until your visa is approved.

5
Collect and double-check your visa

Check: your name is spelled correctly, the dates match your trip, the number of entries (single or double/multiple). Report any errors immediately.

💡 Kat's Tip
Apply at least 3 weeks before your trip — not 3 days. Even if standard processing is 4–7 days, things can go wrong. Give yourself a buffer. I've seen friends scramble at the last minute and it's stressful.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I extend my visa-free stay inside China? ▼
No — visa-free stays cannot be extended. If you want to stay longer than 30 days, you must exit China and re-enter (this works, but check the policy for your country), or apply for a proper visa before you travel. Don't overstay — the penalties are serious.
Can I enter China twice on visa-free? ▼
Yes, generally. The 30-day visa-free policy allows multiple entries. You can exit to Hong Kong, Macau, or another country and re-enter — though there's no official limit, immigration officers may question very frequent re-entries. A standard "exit → Hong Kong trip → re-enter" is totally normal and accepted.
How much does a China tourist visa cost? ▼
Fees vary by country due to reciprocity policies. For US citizens: typically $185 USD for a single-entry L visa. For UK citizens: around £151. Australian citizens: approximately AUD $130. Check the current fee at your local Chinese visa centre — prices change and vary by country.
What's the 240-hour transit visa-free? Can I use it even if I'm not really "in transit"? ▼
You must have a confirmed onward ticket to a third country (not back to your home country). BUT — and this is key — you can use it to genuinely explore China for up to 10 days, then continue to your "third country" destination. Many travelers use New York → Beijing → Tokyo routes and spend the full 10 days in China. Totally legitimate and very popular.
Do I need to book a hotel before applying? ▼
For visa-free entry: no booking required in advance. For visa applications: yes, you typically need hotel reservations as part of your application. They don't need to be paid and non-refundable — a confirmed reservation (with free cancellation) is usually sufficient.
Is Hainan different from the rest of China? ▼
Yes! Hainan island has a separate 30-day visa-free policy for citizens of 59 countries (wider than the mainland list). If your country isn't on the mainland 30-day list, check if Hainan's policy covers you — it might open the door to a China beach holiday without a visa.
My visa application was rejected. What now? ▼
Don't panic. Rejections often happen due to incomplete documents, insufficient funds shown, or unclear travel plans. You can re-apply immediately with stronger documentation. Contact the visa centre to understand the reason. Consider using a reputable visa agency for the reapplication — they know exactly what the consulate wants to see.
